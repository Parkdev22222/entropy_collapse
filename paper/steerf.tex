\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) reliably drives a policy's
entropy toward zero, and a growing family of methods intervenes by re-weighting the
policy loss with a per-token estimate of how much each token's update costs in
entropy. We show that every such method estimates only half of the relevant
quantity. The gradient of the \emph{trajectory} entropy splits exactly into two
terms: a \emph{local} channel, which asks how the conditional distribution at a
visited state flattens or sharpens, and a \emph{visitation} channel, which asks how
the probability of \emph{reaching} that state changes. Existing token-level
estimators capture the first and are structurally blind to the second. The blindness
is worse than an omission: the local estimator is exactly zero at a uniform branch
point, that zero is the correct first-order answer, and the visitation term's
prefactor is simultaneously maximal there --- the two channels are
\emph{anti-correlated}, so no refinement of the local term can recover the missing
signal. We derive the missing channel into a per-token advantage $\AH$ whose
reward-to-go is the remaining conditional entropy $\Htogo$. Because RLVR updates
only after a rollout is complete, $\Htogo$ can be read off the entropies the policy
actually had along the sampled continuation, so the method, STEER-V, needs no
auxiliary model; a learned multi-token forecaster of the same quantity is evaluated
as an ablation. The sibling-prefix baseline that keeps the estimator unbiased is
supplied by a tree-structured rollout, and because $\AH$ sums to zero within a
sibling set, the correction cannot change the average amount of damping --- only
which of two siblings is damped. We test these consequences with LoRA fine-tuning of
a $1.5$B mathematics model in a six-arm design that runs the full pipeline with
$\AH$'s value destroyed in two different ways and with the forecaster in its place,
at three seeds, under an analysis fixed before the first run. Switching the damping
apparatus moves the aggregate entropy by $\num{Csteergrpoentropy}$ and
$\num{Cuniformsteerentropy}$, and changing only what $\AH$ carries moves it by
$\num{Csigneduniformentropy}$ --- a separation the registered test calls
\emph{\num{Dissocverdict}}. On six benchmarks at the final checkpoint, STEER-V leads
the value-destroyed control on $\num{Dirconsistency}$ of six, which the
pre-registered thresholds call \emph{\num{Dirverdict}}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Policy-gradient fine-tuning with verifiable rewards sharpens a language model's
output distribution. Measured as the mean per-token entropy of the policy, this
sharpening is fast, monotone, and, past a point, coincident with the end of useful
learning. The standard reading is that the collapse is the problem, and the standard
remedy is to spend part of the update budget slowing it down: an entropy bonus, a KL
anchor, a clip on the tokens that lose the most entropy, or --- in the most recent
line of work --- a per-token re-weighting of the policy loss by a first-order
estimate of the entropy each token's update destroys.

We argue that the accounting behind that remedy is incomplete, and that the
incompleteness is structural rather than a matter of estimator quality.

Write $\Htraj(\pit \mid x)$ for the entropy of the distribution the policy induces
over whole responses to a prompt $x$. The autoregressive factorization turns it into
an expectation of a sum of per-token conditional entropies, and the gradient of that
expression splits, by the product rule, into exactly two terms. The first holds the
visited states fixed and asks how the conditional distribution at each one changes:
this is what a per-token entropy estimate measures. The second holds the per-state
entropies fixed and asks how the probability of \emph{visiting} each state changes.
An update that concentrates mass on one continuation makes the states downstream of
that continuation more likely and the states downstream of its abandoned siblings
less likely; the diversity those siblings carried leaves the trajectory distribution
even though no conditional distribution anywhere became more peaked.

Our first contribution is to show that the local channel is not merely blind to the
second term but \emph{anti-correlated} with it. The local estimator factors into the
advantage, a saturation factor, and the deviation of the sampled token's surprisal
from the mean surprisal --- and that last factor has mean zero by the definition of
entropy. At a branch point where several continuations are close to equiprobable it
vanishes identically. The vanishing is not an estimator failure: a uniform
distribution maximizes entropy, so the first-order change is genuinely zero and the
damage is second order (Section~\ref{sec:blind}). Meanwhile the visitation term's
prefactor is $1-\pit(y_t \mid s_t)$, which is maximal precisely at those flat
states. The local channel therefore spends its budget where the conditional entropy
is fragile --- rare tokens sampled from confident distributions --- and spends
nothing where the trajectory entropy is most at risk.

Our second contribution is an estimator for the missing channel that requires no
additional rollouts and no auxiliary model. Eliminating the non-causal cross terms
turns the visitation term into a REINFORCE estimator whose reward-to-go is the sum of
the \emph{remaining} conditional entropies, $\Htogo$; subtracting any function of the
state leaves it unbiased. In RLVR the update is made only after the rollout has
finished, and the trainer already evaluates the policy's conditional entropy at every
position of the sampled response, so $\Htogo$ --- truncated and discounted --- is
available for free along the continuation the rollout actually took. We call the
method that uses it STEER-V, for the visitation channel. The state-only baseline comes
from rollouts that share a prefix. Plain group-relative rollouts almost never share
prefixes, so we schedule the sharing: a tree-structured sampler branches a single root
at fixed depths, and the resulting sibling sets give the baseline an actual support
(Section~\ref{sec:tree}). A parallel multi-token-prediction (MTP) forecaster of the
same reward-to-go is the natural alternative, and we run it as an ablation rather than
assume either estimator is the better one (Section~\ref{sec:res:forecaster}).

The resulting per-token score $\AH$ is a deviation from a sibling mean and therefore
sums to zero over each sibling set. This has a consequence we state up front because
it determines what evidence can count: the method \emph{cannot} raise the average
weight at branch points, only spread the weights of siblings apart. ``Total entropy
went up'' is therefore not a prediction of this method, and an experiment looking
for it is testing a different hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:zerosum}).

The experiments fine-tune with LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}. The reason is compute, and we
say so rather than dress it as a design principle: it is what lets every arm run at
three seeds, with its ablations, inside a fixed budget. Every arm --- the baselines
included --- is trained with the identical adapter configuration, so the claims are
comparisons between methods under LoRA; whether they carry over to full fine-tuning
is not claimed here. The analysis, including what would count against us and a check
of whether entropy collapses under LoRA at all, was committed before the first run
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}).

We make the following contributions.

\begin{itemize}
\item An exact two-channel decomposition of the trajectory-entropy gradient, and an
      argument that the channel captured by current token-level entropy-control
      methods is anti-correlated with the channel they miss
      (Sections~\ref{sec:channels}--\ref{sec:blind}).
\item A derivation of the missing channel into a per-token advantage $\AH$ with no
      approximation up to the point of estimating $\Htogo$, an estimator of it that
      reads the realised continuation and adds no parameters, and an explicit ledger
      of the approximations, the sign of each one's error, and the mechanism that
      absorbs it (Sections~\ref{sec:ahdef}--\ref{sec:ledger}).
\item A tree-structured rollout that supplies the sibling-prefix baseline, and a
      bound showing what fraction of positions can carry a non-zero $\AH$ under
      \emph{any} sampler (Section~\ref{sec:tree}).
\item A six-arm experiment that separates the contribution of $\AH$'s \emph{value}
      from that of the apparatus delivering it, by running the full pipeline twice
      with the value destroyed in two different ways, and that tests the estimator
      by running the same method with a learned forecaster in its place
      (Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and~\ref{sec:results}).
\item A test of the dissociation the construction predicts --- that switching the
      damping apparatus moves the aggregate entropy while changing only what $\AH$
      carries does not --- registered in advance as a comparison between groups of
      contrasts rather than read off any single one (Section~\ref{sec:res:entropy}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Entropy collapse in RLVR.}
Entropy decay under policy-gradient fine-tuning is well documented, and a range of
interventions add an explicit entropy term to the objective or constrain the update
so that high-entropy positions are preserved. Recent analyses report that naive
entropy regularization does not prevent the collapse and that continuing to train
under it degrades accuracy \citep{rethinkingentropyreg}, which is consistent with
our reading: the regularizer acts on the local channel, and the local channel is
not where trajectory diversity is being lost.

\paragraph{Assumption.}
Both \citet{steer} and this work rely on one structural assumption, which we restate
so that our approximation ledger can refer to it.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Assumption 1} (parameter-independent softmax; \citealp{steer}).
\label{ass:indep}
For any state $s$, each action $a$ carries an independent logit $z_{s,a}(\theta)$, so
that a gradient step on the sampled token does not substantially move the logits of
the other tokens at that state.
\end{quote}

\noindent
It is false for a network with shared parameters. We inherit it rather than
introduce it, but our construction adds a second estimator that rests on it.

\paragraph{Token-level entropy control.}
The method we build on re-weights the clipped surrogate loss by a per-token
first-order estimate of the entropy change that token's update induces
\citep{steer}. That work observes that earlier interventions are ``indirect'' and
that their effect is therefore limited and can fail. Our decomposition supplies a
mechanism for that observation: the estimate is a first-order estimate of the local
channel only, and it is exactly zero at the states where the visitation channel is
largest.

\paragraph{Which tokens carry the gradient.}
\citet{wang2025dbeyond} show that restricting policy-gradient updates to a small
minority of high-entropy ``forking'' tokens matches full-gradient training. That
result establishes that the identity of the tokens, not the aggregate entropy,
is what matters, and it motivates a per-token treatment; it does not, however,
distinguish the two channels, since forking tokens are identified by their local
entropy. Our analysis predicts that a local criterion will select a systematically
different set from the one the visitation channel would select.

\paragraph{Local stochasticity without global diversity.}
\citet{wu2025ainvisible} report that token-level entropy can remain high while the
diversity of final answers falls. This is precisely the signature our decomposition
predicts when the visitation channel is unmanaged: per-state entropies are
preserved --- the local channel is doing its job --- while the trajectory
distribution concentrates.

\paragraph{Estimating a reward-to-go of entropies.}
The quantity our estimator needs is a sum of future conditional entropies. We read it
off the sampled continuation, which is what a REINFORCE estimator does with any
reward-to-go. The alternative we compare against predicts it from the current hidden
state with Medusa-style parallel prediction heads \citep{medusa}, used not for their
intended purpose (speculative decoding) but as an entropy forecaster; Section~\ref{sec:approx}
shows that forecast has a signed, monotone error.

\paragraph{Parameter-efficient RL fine-tuning.}
Low-rank adaptation \citep{hu2022lora} trains a small number of added parameters
beside a frozen base model and is widely used to fine-tune language models with
reinforcement learning under a limited budget. We use it for that reason. A low-rank
update constrains how the policy can move, which may itself change how quickly it
sharpens; we therefore measure whether entropy collapses under our configuration
before reading anything else (Section~\ref{sec:res:collapse}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Notation.}
A prompt is $x$, a response is $y = y_{1:T}$, and the state before emitting token
$t$ is $s_t = (x, y_{<t})$. We write $H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$ for the entropy of
the conditional distribution at a single position (lowercase, per-token) and
$\Htraj(\pit \mid x)$ for the entropy of the distribution over whole responses
(uppercase, per-trajectory). The surprisal of a token $a$ is
$I_a := -\log \pit(a \mid s_t)$, so that $H = \E_{a \sim \pit}[I_a]$.

\paragraph{Group-relative policy optimization.}
We optimize with GRPO. For each prompt, $n$ responses are sampled and scored, and
each response receives the group-standardized advantage
$A_i = (r_i - \mathrm{mean}_j r_j) / \mathrm{std}_j r_j$, broadcast to every token
of that response. Replacing a learned value function with the group mean is what
makes GRPO cheaper than PPO; it also means that a group whose members all receive
the same reward contributes no gradient.

\paragraph{Token-level entropy control.}
\citet{steer} leave the advantage untouched and instead multiply each token's
contribution to the clipped surrogate by a weight
$\alpha_t \in [\lambda_{\min}, 1]$ derived from a first-order estimate of the
entropy change that token's update induces. Their Theorem~1 gives that estimate in
closed form: under a parameter-independent softmax
(their Assumption~1; our~\ref{ass:indep}), the change in the conditional entropy at
a \emph{fixed} state $s$ after one GRPO step is, to first order,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Om(s) = -\frac{\eta}{L}\, \E_{a}\Bigl[
  &\frac{\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{clip}}(s,a)\, A(s,a)}{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(a \mid s)}\\
  &\cdot\, \pit(a \mid s)\bigl(1 - \pit(a \mid s)\bigr)\\
  &\cdot\, \bigl(\log \pit(a \mid s) + H\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
%
where $\eta$ is the learning rate, $L$ the total decoded length in the update, and
$\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{clip}}$ the PPO clipping indicator. The implementation
evaluates the bracket at the sampled token rather than in expectation. The policy
loss becomes $\sum_u \alpha_u \cdot \mathrm{PPO\text{-}clip}(A_u, r_u)$, with
%
\begin{equation}
\alpha_u \;=\; \exp\!\Bigl(-\alpha\, \frac{|\Om_u|}{\max_v |\Om_v|}\Bigr),
\qquad \lambda_{\min} = e^{-\alpha},
\label{eq:steermap}
\end{equation}
%
a batch-normalized exponential map into $[\lambda_{\min}, 1]$, so the weight can
only attenuate. The factor $1/\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} will
matter later: it makes $\Om$ heavy-tailed, which is why the two channels must be
normalized before they are combined (Section~\ref{sec:combine}).

\section{Two Channels}
\label{sec:channels}

\subsection{Step 1: the chain rule}

The object we would like to protect is the entropy of the distribution the policy
induces over \emph{whole responses},
%
\begin{equation}
\Htraj(\pit \mid x) := -\sum_{y} \pit(y \mid x)\, \log \pit(y \mid x),
\label{eq:Hdef}
\end{equation}
%
a sum over an exponentially large set. The autoregressive factorization
$\pit(y \mid x) = \prod_t \pit(y_t \mid s_t)$ turns the logarithm into a sum and
lets us exchange the order of summation:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Htraj
&= -\sum_{y} \pit(y \mid x) \sum_{t=1}^{T} \log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\\
&= \sum_{t=1}^{T} \E_{y \sim \pit}\bigl[-\log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\bigr].
\end{split}
\label{eq:chainstep}
\end{equation}
%
Each summand is still an expectation over whole responses, but the integrand depends
on $y$ only through $y_{\le t}$. Conditioning on the prefix and applying the tower
rule replaces the inner expectation with a per-position entropy,
%
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
\E_{y}&\bigl[-\log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\bigr]\\
&= \E_{y_{<t}}\Bigl[\,\underbrace{\E_{y_t \sim \pit(\cdot \mid s_t)}\bigl[-\log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\bigr]}_{= \; H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))}\Bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
%
because the inner expectation is, by definition, the entropy of the conditional
distribution at $s_t$. Collecting the terms back under a single expectation gives
%
\begin{equation}
\Htraj(\pit \mid x)
= \E_{y \sim \pit}\Bigl[\sum_{t=1}^{T} H\bigl(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t)\bigr)\Bigr].
\label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:chain} is an identity, not an approximation. Its left-hand side
is what we want to protect; its right-hand side is what instrumentation can measure,
since $H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$ is read directly off the logits at each position.

The entire argument of this paper follows from one feature of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain} that is easy to pass over: \emph{the measure
$\E_{y \sim \pit}$ depends on $\theta$ as well}. The right-hand side is not a sum of
$\theta$-dependent terms over a fixed set of positions --- it is a $\theta$-dependent
average of them over a $\theta$-dependent set of trajectories.

\subsection{Step 2: the product rule}

Write the per-trajectory total
%
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(y) := \sum_{t=1}^{T} H\bigl(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t(y))\bigr),
\label{eq:fdef}
\end{equation}
%
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain} reads
$\Htraj = \sum_y \pit(y \mid x)\, f_\theta(y)$. Both factors of every summand carry
$\theta$, so differentiating requires the product rule:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\gradt \Htraj
&= \sum_y \bigl[\gradt \pit(y \mid x)\bigr] f_\theta(y)\\
&\quad + \sum_y \pit(y \mid x)\, \gradt f_\theta(y).
\end{split}
\label{eq:productrule}
\end{equation}
%
The second sum is already an expectation. The first is not, because
$\gradt \pit$ is not a probability distribution; the score-function identity
$\gradt \pit = \pit \gradt \log \pit$ restores it to one. Substituting and naming
the two terms,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\gradt \Htraj
&= \underbrace{\E\bigl[\gradt f_\theta(y)\bigr]}_{(\mathrm{L})\ \text{local}}\\
&\quad + \underbrace{\E\bigl[f_\theta(y)\,\gradt \log \pit(y \mid x)\bigr]}_{(\mathrm{V})\ \text{visitation}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:twochannel}
\end{equation}
%
Equations~\eqref{eq:Hdef}--\eqref{eq:twochannel} contain no approximation of any
kind. The split into two channels is forced by the product rule, not chosen.

\subsection{Reading the two terms}

\textbf{(L), the local channel}, holds the visited states fixed and asks how the
conditional distribution at each one changes. Equation~\eqref{eq:omega} is a
first-order estimate of exactly this.

\textbf{(V), the visitation channel}, holds the per-state entropies fixed and asks
how the probability of reaching each state changes. An update that concentrates mass
on one continuation makes the sub-tree below it more likely and the sub-trees below
its siblings less likely. Nothing in Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} can see this: $\Om$ reads
the conditional distribution at a position and never reads the probability of
arriving there.

The two terms can have opposite signs. A method that drives (L) to zero has not
thereby bounded $\gradt \Htraj$.

\subsection{Why the local channel cannot be repaired}
\label{sec:blind}

A natural objection is that the two channels might coincide in practice: branch
points are positions where the policy is uncertain, so they are high-entropy
positions, so $\Om$ should be large there and the local term should protect them
automatically. The premise is correct --- in warm-up rollouts on our model, branch
diversity correlates with local entropy at Spearman $\rho = 0.65$, and with the
entropy-to-go at $\rho = 0.82$. The inference from it fails, in three steps.

\paragraph{Step 1: $\Om$ reads a deviation, not a level.}
Collecting the constants of Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} into
$c := \eta\,\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{clip}} A / (L\,\pi_{\mathrm{old}})$ and rewriting in
terms of surprisal gives, for the contribution of a single action $a$,
%
\begin{equation}
\Om \;\propto\; c\;\pi_a(1-\pi_a)\,\bigl(H - I_a\bigr),
\label{eq:omega-surprisal}
\end{equation}
%
and $H = \E_{a\sim\pi}[I_a]$ by definition. The factor $H - I_a$ is therefore a
mean-zero fluctuation: what sets the magnitude of $|\Om|$ is the \emph{spread} of
surprisal, not the \emph{height} of the entropy. Nothing in $c$ depends on the shape
of $\pi(\cdot \mid s)$, so the argument below is unaffected by the clipping
indicator or the importance weight.

\paragraph{Step 2: at a uniform branch point $\Om \equiv 0$.}
If the distribution is uniform over $k$ candidates then $I_a = \log k = H$ for every
$a$, so $\Om$ vanishes identically. The positions that are most branch-like --- several
continuations at comparable probability --- are exactly where the local term
contributes nothing.

\paragraph{Step 3: and that zero is the correct answer.}
This is the step that closes the argument, and it follows from
\citeauthor{steer}'s own derivation. Differentiating the conditional entropy with
respect to a single logit gives \citep[Eq.~20]{steer}
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\frac{\partial H(\pit \mid s)}{\partial z_{s,a}}
= -\,\pit(a \mid s)\bigl(&\log \pit(a \mid s)\\[-2pt]
  &+ H(\pit \mid s)\bigr),
\end{split}
\label{eq:dHdz}
\end{equation}
%
and at a uniform distribution $\log \pit(a \mid s) = -\log k = -H$ makes the bracket
vanish for \emph{every} $a$. So $\partial H / \partial z_a = 0$ identically: $\Om$
is not wrong, it is right. Perturbing one logit of a uniform distribution by $\delta$ and writing
$S := e^{\delta} + k - 1$ gives the closed form
%
\begin{equation}
H(\delta) = \log S - \frac{\delta e^{\delta}}{S},
\qquad
H''(0) = -\,\frac{k-1}{k^{2}},
\label{eq:secondorder}
\end{equation}
%
with $H'(\delta)$ of sign $-\operatorname{sign}(\delta)$: any push in any direction
lowers the entropy, but the loss is entirely second order. For a $10$-way uniform
distribution and $\delta = 0.1$ the actual entropy loss is $4.7\times10^{-4}$,
roughly two orders of magnitude below that of a peaked distribution under the same
perturbation. \emph{As far as local entropy is concerned, uniform branch points are
genuinely safe}, and a local estimator that allocates them no protection is
allocating correctly for its own objective. No refinement of the local term
recovers the missing signal, because the missing signal is not local.

\paragraph{The two channels are anti-correlated.}
The visitation term's prefactor, derived in Section~\ref{sec:weights}, is
$1 - \pi_a$, which approaches $1$ as the distribution flattens --- and the
probability that a group of $n$ samples actually diverges at a position approaches
$1$ in the same limit. Table~\ref{tab:anticorr} evaluates both channels on a family
of distributions that interpolates between confident and flat. $|\Om|$ is dominated
by positions where the model is fairly confident but a tail token was sampled; at
those positions siblings diverge only about half the time. Where divergence is
certain, the local term is identically zero and the visitation prefactor is maximal.

\label{tab:anticorr}
\end{table}

The statement we can therefore make is stronger than ``the local term is
incomplete'': \emph{the local term is anti-correlated with the term it omits}, and
the omission cannot be fixed within the local channel.

\section{Estimating the Visitation Channel}
\label{sec:ahdef}

\subsection{Causal elimination gives a reward-to-go}

The visitation term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:twochannel} is not yet in a usable form: the
factor $f_\theta(y)$ multiplying the score is a property of the \emph{whole}
trajectory, so a naive estimator would credit every token with entropy that appeared
before it. Causality removes those terms exactly.

Expanding both factors --- $f_\theta$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:fdef} and the score by
$\gradt \log \pit(y \mid x) = \sum_u \gradt \log \pit(y_u \mid s_u)$ --- turns (V)
into a double sum:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(\mathrm{V}) = \sum_t \sum_u
\E\bigl[&H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))\\
&\cdot \gradt \log \pit(y_u \mid s_u)\bigr].
\end{split}
\label{eq:doublesum}
\end{equation}
%
Consider a term with $u \ge t$. The entropy $H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$ depends on $y$
only through $y_{<t}$, and $y_{<t} \subseteq y_{<u}$, so it is measurable with
respect to $y_{<u}$ and may be pulled out of a conditional expectation given
$y_{<u}$. What remains is the expected score at a single position, which vanishes
for any distribution:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\E\bigl[\gradt &\log \pit(y_u \mid s_u) \mid y_{<u}\bigr]\\
&= \sum_a \pit(a \mid s_u) \frac{\gradt \pit(a \mid s_u)}{\pit(a \mid s_u)}\\
&= \gradt \!\sum_a \pit(a \mid s_u)
\;=\; \gradt 1 \;=\; 0 .
\end{split}
\label{eq:scorezero}
\end{equation}
%
By the tower rule every $(t, u)$ term with $u \ge t$ is therefore \emph{exactly}
zero --- not small, zero. This is the same identity that licenses a baseline in
policy gradients, used here to enforce causality rather than to reduce variance.

Only $u < t$ survives. Swapping the order of summation collects, for each token
$u$, the entropies at all later positions:
%
\begin{equation}
(\mathrm{V}) = \E\Bigl[\sum_u \gradt \log \pit(y_u \mid s_u)\,
\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u)\Bigr],
\label{eq:togo}
\end{equation}
%
with $\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) := \sum_{t > u} H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$.

We stress that $\Htogo$ was not posited. Equation~\eqref{eq:togo} is a REINFORCE
estimator whose reward is ``the conditional entropy remaining after this token'',
and $\Htogo$ is that reward-to-go.

\subsection{The sibling-prefix baseline}

Any function $b(s_u)$ of the state alone leaves Eq.~\eqref{eq:togo} unbiased, by the
same argument. Subtracting one gives the per-token advantage
%
\begin{equation}
\AH(s_u, y_u) := \Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) - \bar{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}(s_u),
\label{eq:ah}
\end{equation}
%
and up to this point \emph{no approximation has been made}.

The baseline we use is the mean of $\Htogo$ over the rollouts in the same prompt
group whose prefixes $y_{<u}$ agree exactly. Those rollouts share the state $s_u$ by
definition, so their mean is a function of $s_u$ alone and the baseline is
admissible. The condition is agreement on $y_{<u}$ and not on $y_{\le u}$: the
rollouts must diverge \emph{at} $u$ for the difference to be a branch score.

Two properties follow immediately and matter later. First, the implementation
includes a rollout in its own sibling set, so $b$ depends weakly on $y_u$ and the
estimator is not strictly leave-one-out; the resulting bias is $O(1/m)$ in the
number $m$ of surviving siblings, the same property GRPO's group-mean advantage
has. Second, when $m = 1$ the baseline equals the rollout's own value and
$\AH \equiv 0$, so a position with no siblings is silent rather than noisy.


\section{Estimating the Reward-to-Go}
\label{sec:approx}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ah} is exact but not computable as written: $\Htogo$ sums the
conditional entropies over the whole remaining response. Two approximations make it
computable, and a third, in Section~\ref{sec:weights}, bounds its influence.

\subsection{A1: horizon truncation and discounting}

We truncate the sum to $\kappa$ steps and discount:
%
\begin{equation}
\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) \approx
\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa} \gamma_H^{\,k}\, H\bigl(\pi(\cdot \mid s_{u+k})\bigr).
\label{eq:a1}
\end{equation}
%
The discount serves two purposes: distant terms are further from the token they are
credited to and add variance without adding much signal, and the sum stays bounded
independently of $\kappa$.

What this discards --- the tail $\sum_{k > \kappa}$ and a fraction of the retained
terms --- is largely harmless for a reason specific to our use. Equation~\eqref{eq:ah}
consumes only the \emph{difference} between siblings. Any bias common to rollouts
sharing $s_u$ cancels in that difference, and truncation bias is common to first
order.

\subsection{A2: one realised continuation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:a1} needs the future states $s_{u+k}$. In RLVR they exist: the
update is made after the rollout has finished, every response in the batch carries the
continuation it actually took, and the trainer evaluates the policy's conditional
entropy $H(\pi(\cdot \mid s_t))$ at every response position in the same forward pass
that produces the log-probabilities. We therefore evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:a1} on the
sampled continuation,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}&(s_u \oplus y_u)\\
&= \sum_{k=1}^{\kappa} \gamma_H^{\,k}\, H\bigl(\pi(\cdot \mid s_{u+k})\bigr),
\end{split}
\label{eq:realised}
\end{equation}
%
where the $s_{u+k}$ are the states of the sampled response.
This is not an approximation of the estimator but the estimator itself: the
reward-to-go of Eq.~\eqref{eq:togo} is a sum over the \emph{same} trajectory whose
score it multiplies, and a REINFORCE estimator uses the sampled trajectory's value.
What A2 names is the price of that: $\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}$ is a single draw of the
continuation, unbiased for the truncated sum and carrying the variance of one sample.
It costs nothing --- no parameters, no warm-up, no forward pass beyond the one the
trainer already makes --- and it needs no alignment shift, because
$s_{u+1} = s_u \oplus y_u$ already reads the state after the branch taken.

Two consequences recur below. The value at $u$ depends on the sampled tokens
$y_{u+1:u+\kappa-1}$ as well as on $y_{\le u}$, which changes where $\AH$ can be non-zero
(Section~\ref{sec:tree}). And the states come from $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$ while the
entropies are the current policy's at those states, the same mismatch B3 records for
every term of the loss.

\subsection{An alternative: forecasting the future}
\label{sec:mtp}

The variance of one continuation can be traded for a bias by predicting the future
instead of reading it. We implement that alternative and run it as an ablation
(Section~\ref{sec:res:forecaster}): parallel heads read the current hidden state $h_u$
and forecast the entropy $k$ steps ahead,
%
\begin{equation}
H\bigl(\pi(\cdot \mid s_{u+k})\bigr) \;\longrightarrow\;
H\bigl(p^{(k)}_{\mathrm{MTP}}(\cdot \mid h_u)\bigr).
\label{eq:a2}
\end{equation}
%
Head $k$ is a two-layer residual MLP applied to $h_u$, followed by the policy's own
unembedding; the heads own no vocabulary parameters. Their last layer is
zero-initialized, so before any training every head reproduces the policy's own
next-token distribution. In the RL path the logits are materialized in chunks and
reduced to an entropy immediately, so the $[\kappa, B, T, V]$ tensor never exists.

\paragraph{M1: the forecast's error has a known sign.}
Conditioning on the intervening tokens is exactly what the forecast omits. Fixing
$S_u$, the $k$-th term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:a1} has expectation
$H(Y_{u+k} \mid Y_{u+1:u+k-1}, S_u)$, whereas the head models the
\emph{marginal} $p(Y_{u+k} \mid S_u)$. Since conditioning reduces entropy,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
H(Y_{u+k} \mid S_u) &- \E\bigl[H(\pi(\cdot \mid S_{u+k-1}))\bigr]\\
&= I\bigl(Y_{u+k};\, Y_{u+1:u+k-1} \mid S_u\bigr)\\
&\ge 0 .
\end{split}
\label{eq:mi}
\end{equation}
%
The forecast \emph{always over-estimates}, and the excess is precisely the mutual
information with the intervening tokens, which grows monotonically with $k$. For
$k = 1$ there are no intervening tokens and the excess vanishes.

\paragraph{M2: per-head affine calibration.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:mi} predicts a per-head, monotone inflation, so the forecast is
corrected by a per-head affine map fitted by least squares against the realised
entropies of warm-up rollouts of the base model,
%
\begin{equation}
\hat{H}_k = a_k\, H\bigl(p^{(k)}_{\mathrm{MTP}}(\cdot \mid h_u)\bigr) + b_k ,
\label{eq:a3}
\end{equation}
%
and the forecast $\max(0, \sum_k \gamma_H^{\,k} \hat{H}_k)$ replaces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:realised}. Its hidden state at position $u$ predicts $y_u$ and does not
know it, so the value for $\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u)$ is read at position $u+1$ and
shifted back by one. The fitted coefficients are released with the heads.

\paragraph{Which one, and why both are run.}
The realised value is the reward-to-go the derivation asks for, sampled once; the
forecast is an estimate of its expectation given $h_u$, lower in variance and biased
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:mi} until M2 removes it. Nothing in the derivation decides which of
those two errors costs more inside a training loop, and a measurement on the base
policy (Section~\ref{sec:res:locality}) finds the calibrated forecast close to a
scalar multiple of the realised next-step term once both are differenced against
siblings. We take the realised value as the method because it is exact where the
forecast is approximate and free where the forecast needs a warm-up and a second
forward pass, and we run the forecaster at every seed so that the choice is measured
rather than assumed.

\section{From Gradient to Token Weights}
\label{sec:weights}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ah} is a term to add to a gradient, but methods in this family do
not add gradients --- they re-weight a loss. What is needed is a first-order
prediction of how much one update, acting through token $u$, changes the trajectory
entropy.

\paragraph{How far one step moves a logit.}
We reuse the logit-space update of \citet[Eq.~23]{steer} rather than rederiving it.
With $w_u = \mathrm{clip}(r_u, 1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}, 1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{hi}})A_u$
the coefficient the policy loss actually applies, Assumption~\ref{ass:indep} makes a
single step move only the sampled token's logit,
$\Delta z_{u,a} = \eta\, w_u\, \delta_{a, y_u} + O(\eta^2)$.
The softmax Jacobian $\partial \log \pi_a / \partial z_a = 1 - \pi_a$ then gives
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u = \eta\, w_u\,(1 - \pi_u),
\label{eq:dlogpi}
\end{equation}
%
which vanishes as $\pi_u \to 1$: a token that already holds the mass cannot be given
more. This is the prefactor whose anti-correlation with $|\Om|$ Table~\ref{tab:anticorr}
records.

\paragraph{The visitation-channel prediction.}
Because $\gradt \log \pi_u \cdot \Delta\theta = \Delta \log \pi_u$, substituting into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ah} gives the per-token quantity
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{visit}_u := \widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u \cdot
\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\AH(s_u,y_u), -c, +c\bigr).
\end{split}
\label{eq:visit}
\end{equation}
%
\textbf{A3} is the clip, and it is applied to $\AH$ \emph{before} the product rather
than after, so that one token's influence is bounded by $|\eta w_u| \cdot c$.
Clipping the product instead would let estimation error be amplified without bound at
tokens with large $w_u$.

\paragraph{Sign quadrants.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:visit} defines the collapse channel precisely.
When $\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u > 0$ and $\AH > 0$, mass moves toward a branch
richer than its siblings and trajectory entropy rises. When
$\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u > 0$ and $\AH < 0$, mass moves into a dead end and the
diversity carried by the abandoned siblings is destroyed --- this is the collapse
path that (L) is structurally unable to see. Negative
$\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u$ reverses both readings.

\section{Combining the Channels}
\label{sec:combine}

The visitation term could be added to $\Om$ before the weight map, but measured on
real rollouts that map destroys the signal: $\Om$ carries a factor
$1/\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$, so $\max|\Om| / \mathrm{median}|\Om|$ reaches $3\times10^{4}$
and the band is spent on one or two outliers while the bulk lands on a point. We
therefore apply the correction \emph{after} the map, as a bounded adjustment of the
weights the base method has already produced.

Let $\mathcal{S} = \{u : \mathrm{visit}_u \neq 0\}$ be the support --- by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branchonly}, the branch points --- and let
$b = \lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min}$ be the width of the weight band. For
$u \in \mathcal{S}$,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\alpha^{\mathrm{f}}_u = \mathrm{clamp}\Bigl(&\alpha_u
  + \lambda\, b\, \tanh\!\bigl(\mathrm{visit}_u / \rho\bigr),\\
  &\lambda_{\min},\ \lambda_{\max}\Bigr),
\end{split}
\label{eq:combine}
\end{equation}
%
with $\rho = \mathrm{rms}\{\mathrm{visit}_v : v \in \mathcal{S}\}$;
$\alpha^{\mathrm{f}}_u = \alpha_u$ elsewhere.

Three properties matter. The $\tanh$ is odd, monotone and bounded, so it preserves
the sign of the visitation term, caps one token's movement at $\lambda b$, and does
not depend on the absolute scale of $\mathrm{visit}$. The RMS is taken over
$\mathcal{S}$ alone rather than over the whole response: normalizing by the whole
response would shrink the correction in proportion to how sparse branch points are,
which is a property of the sampler and not of the signal. And at $\lambda = 0$ the
correction vanishes identically, so the pipeline is bitwise identical to the base
method --- a regression test enforces this.

Because the map runs first and the correction second, the step size $\eta$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dlogpi} enters only through $\mathrm{visit}_u / \rho$, where it
cancels. We fix $\eta = 1$ and sweep $\lambda$ alone.

\paragraph{The support is part of the treatment.}
$\mathcal{S}$ is defined by $\mathrm{visit}_u \neq 0$ rather than by a threshold,
deliberately: it selects both where the correction lands and, through $\rho$, how
large every correction is, so a tighter test would change what is trained rather than
what is logged. One consequence is that $\mathcal{S}$ admits float32 round-off ---
$\AH$ is a deviation from a mean over token-identical siblings, and
$\sum_j g_j / m$ does not reproduce $g_i$ bitwise, leaving $|\AH| \sim 6\times10^{-8}$
where it is interpretively zero. Those residuals are $\sim\!10^{-6}$ relative to a
real branch score and cannot move a weight, but they are counted in the support
statistics of Section~\ref{sec:res:diag}, which should be read as an upper bound on
the branch-point fraction.

\section{Tree-Structured Rollouts}
\label{sec:tree}

\subsection{Why plain rollouts cannot support $\AH$}

$\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}(s_u \oplus y_u)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:realised} reads the states
$s_{u+1}, \dots, s_{u+\kappa}$, so it is a deterministic function of the prefix
$y_{\le u+\kappa-1}$, and two rollouts that share that prefix produce identical values.
Combined with Eq.~\eqref{eq:ah}, whose baseline averages over rollouts sharing
$y_{<u}$, this gives a sharp characterization:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\AH[i, u] \neq 0 \implies\ &\text{siblings share } y_{<u}\\
&\text{but differ in } y_{u:u+\kappa-1},
\end{split}
\label{eq:branchonly}
\end{equation}
%
that is, $u$ is a branch point of the group or lies within $\kappa - 1$ positions
before one. Under independent sampling from a sharpened policy, group members diverge
at their first token and never share a prefix again, so the sibling set is the
rollout alone almost everywhere and $\AH \equiv 0$: the visitation channel is not
merely weak, it is identically absent.

The $\kappa - 1$ positions before a branch are specific to the realised estimator,
and they are worth naming. There the siblings still emit the same token from the same
state, and $\AH$ credits that one token, in each sibling, with a divergence that
happens after it; the forecast of Section~\ref{sec:mtp}, a function of $y_{\le u}$
alone, is zero there. With $\kappa = 2$ it is one position per branch, weighted by
$\gamma_H^{2}$ against $\gamma_H$ for the branch token itself.

We therefore schedule the sharing. A tree sampler expands a single root and cuts it
at fixed depths $d_1 < d_2 < d_3$ with branching factor $2$, yielding $n = 8$ leaves
whose sibling structure is known in advance. The cuts create the only positions at
which Eq.~\eqref{eq:branchonly} can fire.

\subsection{A ceiling on the support}

It is tempting to treat the fraction of positions with $\AH \neq 0$ as a quantity to
be maximized. It is not, and the reason is combinatorial rather than empirical. Two
quantities behave very differently under the tree:

\begin{itemize}
\item the fraction of positions where \emph{some} other rollout shares $y_{<u}$ is
      moved by the cuts, and equals $d_L/T$ by construction;
\item the fraction where $\AH \neq 0$ is \emph{not}, because by
      Eq.~\eqref{eq:branchonly} it counts positions at or just before branch points,
      and $n$ sequences form a trie with at most $n-1$ internal branching nodes.
\end{itemize}

Each branch node contributes at most $\kappa$ positions, so the fraction of positions
carrying a non-zero $\AH$ is bounded by $\kappa(n-1)/T$ for \emph{any} sampler
whatsoever; with $\kappa = 2$ this is the same $2(n-1)/T$ that bounds the forecast
variant, whose one-position alignment shift widens each branch node into two columns.
With $n = 8$ and responses of $\sim 10^3$ tokens the ceiling is on the order of a
percent. The tree's role is to make the branch points exist and to place them at
controlled depths, not to make them numerous.

\subsection{The correction cannot move the mean}
\label{sec:zerosum}

$\AH$ is a deviation from a sibling mean, so on each sibling set
%
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j \in \mathrm{sib}(s_u)} \AH\bigl(s_u, y^{(j)}_u\bigr) = 0 .
\label{eq:zerosum}
\end{equation}
%
The correction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:combine} therefore cannot change the average weight
assigned to branch tokens. It can only spread siblings apart. This is not a defect
to be engineered away: an unbiased estimator requires a baseline, and a baseline
forces the mean to zero.

The consequence for evaluation is direct, and we state it before presenting any
experiment. ``Mean entropy at branch points increased'' is \emph{not} a prediction of
this method and cannot serve as evidence that it works; the corresponding prediction
is that the \emph{variance across siblings} widens. A method designed to raise the
mean is a different and weaker hypothesis --- it does not need $\AH$'s value at all,
only a support --- and we include it below as a control arm.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Model, data, and optimization}

All runs fine-tune the same $1.5$B-parameter mathematics-specialized base model on the
same $17$k-problem competition-mathematics set, with the same optimizer settings, and
differ only in the intervention. Every run trains for $150$ optimization steps; with a
training batch of $512$ prompts that is about $4.4$ epochs. Two further backbones carry
the headline contrast only (Section~\ref{sec:backbones}).

\paragraph{Low-rank adaptation.} Every arm, the baselines included, trains a LoRA
adapter \citep{hu2022lora} of rank $64$ and scale $\alpha = 32$ on every linear
projection of the attention and MLP blocks; the embedding and the unembedding stay
frozen, so the unembedding the forecaster of Section~\ref{sec:mtp} reads is the base
model's. The learning rate is $1\mathrm{e}{-5}$, constant, with no warm-up --- ten
times the rate full fine-tuning of this model would use, the usual ratio for LoRA,
fixed before any run and identical across arms. The reason for LoRA is compute, not a
property we claim for it: it is what allows every arm three seeds inside our budget.

\paragraph{Policy optimization.} GRPO with $n = 8$ responses per prompt, a maximum
response length of $3072$ tokens, no entropy bonus and no KL term. The clipped
surrogate is asymmetric, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.2$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.28$, with the dual-clip bound at $c = 10$; we state
this because ``GRPO'' with a symmetric $\varepsilon$ and a KL term is a different
baseline, and because the follow-up arm of Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate} is defined by
moving these numbers. Rollouts are sampled at temperature $1.0$ without nucleus
truncation. Each Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B run trains on a single H100 (80\,GB), four runs
side by side on one node, with the rollout engine's CUDA graphs enabled; the
backbone runs of Section~\ref{sec:backbones} use the whole four-GPU node. Every
arm of a table shares its layout, which each released log records.

\paragraph{Validation and checkpoints.} Every $10$ steps each run is validated on
AIME 2024 ($30$ problems, $32$ samples each, temperature $1.0$, top-$p$ $0.7$). A
single such measurement has an across-problem standard error of about
$\num{SEaime}$, which is why no statistic below rests on one validation point. Each
run keeps its checkpoints at steps $50$, $100$ and $150$ and at its best AIME24
validation; the last is the primary evaluation point and the best a secondary one
(Section~\ref{sec:endpoints}).

\subsection{Method hyperparameters}

$\Htogo$ is truncated at $\kappa = 2$ with $\gamma_H = 0.7$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:realised}).
The combination uses $\lambda = 0.25$, RMS normalization, clip $c = 1.0$, and the
sibling-prefix baseline. The weight map is the base method's exponential map of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:steermap} at $\lambda_{\min} = 0.7$, the value its authors report, so
weights land in $[0.7, 1]$ and can only attenuate; its released script sets $0.8$, and
Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate} runs that too. Every weighted arm uses the same band. The
tree sampler uses a single root, cuts at depths $\{64, 192, 384\}$, and branching
factor $2$ at each cut, giving $n = 8$ leaves. The forecaster arm uses two heads with
the calibration of Eq.~\eqref{eq:a3}, fitted once on warm-up rollouts of the base model.

The design isolates two questions. Does the \emph{value} of $\AH$ matter, or only the
apparatus that carries it? A comparison between the base method and the full method
changes several things at once (the correction and the sampler), so it cannot answer
this; we therefore add two arms that keep the entire apparatus --- the tree, the
support, the weight map --- and destroy only the information in $\AH$. And does the
estimator of $\Htogo$ matter? A sixth arm keeps everything and replaces the realised
value with the forecast.

\textsc{uniform} applies the same attenuation to every position in the support,

\subsection{Endpoints, fixed before the first run}
\label{sec:endpoints}

The analysis below was committed before any run of this campaign
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}); the seed is the unit of analysis throughout.

\paragraph{Primary.} Accuracy on six mathematics benchmarks --- AIME24, AIME25,
AMC23, MATH500, Minerva and OlympiadBench --- at $32$ samples per problem, at the last
training step of each run. No checkpoint is selected, so AIME24 is as held-out as the

the six a contrast favours the treatment, averaged over seeds --- four or more is
support, three equivocal, two or fewer evidence against --- together with the paired
across-problem difference on MATH500, OlympiadBench and Minerva, whose sizes support
one.

\paragraph{Secondary.} The same evaluation at each run's AIME24-best checkpoint, where
AIME24 is in-sample; and AIME24 accuracy averaged over the validation points of steps
$40$--$150$ (the \emph{converged window}), which is what the training logs carry at
every seed.

\paragraph{Contrasts.} STEER-V against GRPO, \textsc{steer}, \textsc{uniform},
\textsc{permuted} and STEER-V (MTP); \textsc{steer} against GRPO; \textsc{uniform}
against \textsc{steer}. The contrasts against \textsc{uniform} and \textsc{permuted}
change one thing each and carry the claim.

\paragraph{What would count against the claim.} (1) STEER-V $-$ \textsc{uniform} is
equivocal or against on the primary endpoint, or straddles zero across seeds: the
value of $\AH$ is not what helps. (2) The same for STEER-V $-$ \textsc{permuted}: the
pairing of a branch with its score is not what helps. (3) The dissociation of
Section~\ref{sec:res:entropy} fails. (4) The mean token weight of STEER-V leaves
$[0.99, 1]$: the arm is a change of learning rate, not a reweighting.

\paragraph{Metrics.} $\mathrm{acc@}32$ is the mean accuracy over $32$ samples and
$\mathrm{maj@}32$ the accuracy of their majority vote; $\mathrm{uplift} :=
\mathrm{maj@}32 - \mathrm{acc@}32$ measures whether correct answers concentrate while
incorrect ones scatter. The logged \texttt{actor/entropy} is a token-mean, i.e.\
$\Htraj$ divided by the mean response length, and every entropy number below is that
quantity.

\subsection{Does entropy collapse under LoRA at all?}
\label{sec:res:collapse}

A low-rank update constrains how the policy can move, and it could slow the
sharpening that collapse describes enough to leave the method nothing to correct. So
the first registered measurement is GRPO's own entropy. Averaged over its
\num{Collapsenseeds} seeds, GRPO's token-mean entropy goes from \num{Collapsestart}
at step $10$ to \num{Collapseend} at step $150$, a ratio of \num{Collapseratio}.
\num{Collapsencollapsed} of \num{Collapsenseeds} seeds fall below half their
step-$10$ value, which is the registered definition of collapse, so under this
configuration GRPO \emph{\num{Collapseverdict}}. If it does not, the method's
motivation by collapse does not apply to this regime, and the rest of this section
is about redistribution under LoRA rather than rescue from collapse.

\subsection{Does the estimator matter?}
\label{sec:res:forecaster}

STEER-V (MTP) keeps everything and replaces the realised $\Htogo$ with the calibrated
forecast of Section~\ref{sec:mtp}. On the converged window, STEER-V $-$ MTP is
$\num{Csignedmtpacc}$ ($t = \num{Tsignedmtpacc}$, $n = \num{Nsignedmtp}$). On the

step takes $\num{Rsignedcost}$\,s for STEER-V and $\num{Rmtpcost}$\,s for the MTP

price the forecast is measured against.

\subsection{Is the forecast a local term?}
\label{sec:res:locality}

A measurement on the base policy, made before any training, explains why the two
estimators could behave alike. We apply the \emph{same} operator --- the same sibling
baseline, mask and grouping --- to the calibrated forecast and to the policy's own
next-token entropy read at the state after the branch token. Over \num{Locn} branch
points from \num{Locgroups} validation prompts, the two advantages correlate at
$r = \num{Locadv}$ (Spearman $\num{Locadvrho}$), the local quantity explains
$R^2 = \num{Locrsq}$ of the forecast's, they agree in sign $\num{Locsign}$ of the time
--- the sign is what the weights are monotone in --- and the magnitudes stand in the
ratio $\num{Locratio}$, close to the first head's calibrated weight times $\gamma_H$.
Once differenced against siblings, the calibrated forecast is very nearly a scalar
multiple of the first term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:realised}, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:combine}
divides scale out through $\rho$. That is a reason to expect the realised estimator to
lose little, not a proof of it; Section~\ref{sec:res:forecaster} is the proof either
way. The measurement is on a frozen policy and independently sampled rollouts, so its
sibling support sits early in the response rather than at the scheduled cuts.

\subsection{Seeds and spread}
\label{sec:res:seeds}

Every statistic above collapses a run to one number over its converged window, pairs
arms within a seed, and tests the seed-level differences with $\mathrm{df} = n - 1$.
Pairing within a seed cancels whatever a seed carries for all arms, the data order
first of all. What it does not cancel is run-to-run spread, and that is the number to
read the contrasts against: GRPO's converged-window accuracy moves from
\num{Logrpoacc} to \num{Higrpoacc} across its seeds, STEER-V's from \num{Losignedacc}
to \num{Hisignedacc}. Rollout sampling is not seeded across processes, so re-running a
configuration is a second draw rather than a restoration.

\subsection{Mechanism diagnostics and cost}
\label{sec:res:diag}
\label{sec:res:cost}

Section~\ref{sec:tree} bounds the fraction of positions that can carry a non-zero
$\AH$ at $\kappa(n-1)/T$ for any sampler. STEER-V measures \num{Rsignedbranchfrac} of
positions with $\AH \neq 0$, at a mean response length of \num{Rsignedlen} tokens;
the count admits float32 round-off at positions where siblings are token-identical,
so it is an upper bound on the branch-point fraction. One optimization step takes
$\num{Rgrpocost}$\,s for GRPO, $\num{Rsteercost}$ for \textsc{steer},
$\num{Runiformcost}$ for \textsc{uniform}, $\num{Rpermutedcost}$ for
\textsc{permuted}, $\num{Rsignedcost}$ for STEER-V and $\num{Rmtpcost}$ for the MTP
variant. The three tree arms that read the realised value differ from one another only
by a subtraction over a mask, so a gap between them and \textsc{steer} is the tree
sampler's cost, and the MTP variant adds its forward pass on top.

\section{Conclusion}

The gradient of trajectory entropy has two terms, and the token-level entropy
controls now in use estimate one of them. The omission is structural: the estimated
term vanishes exactly where the omitted term is largest, and it vanishes for a correct
reason, so the gap cannot be closed by improving the estimator. The omitted term is a
REINFORCE estimator whose reward-to-go is the remaining conditional entropy; in RLVR
that reward-to-go can be read off the rollout the update is already made from, and a
tree-structured rollout supplies the sibling baseline that makes it estimable at all.
The resulting correction is mean-preserving by construction: it does not slow entropy
collapse, it redirects it.

The experiment is built to test those consequences rather than a gain. It separates
the correction's value from its apparatus with two controls that keep the apparatus
and destroy the value; it tests the dissociation the construction predicts as a
comparison between groups of contrasts; it runs the learned forecaster beside the
realised estimator; and it first asks whether entropy collapses under LoRA at all.
What each of those returned is in Section~\ref{sec:results}, read by rules fixed
before the first run.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{LoRA only.} Every run trains a rank-$64$ LoRA adapter. We chose it for
compute, every arm uses the identical configuration, and the claims are comparisons
between methods under it. Whether they carry over to full fine-tuning is not claimed.
Full fine-tuning pilots of an earlier version of this method exist; they are not
pooled with anything here and no statement in this paper rests on them.

\paragraph{Three seeds, one benchmark family in training.} Every core arm has three
seeds, which bounds the effect sizes rather than the structural test: the
dissociation is a comparison between two groups of contrasts, and no single row
carries it. Validation during training is AIME 2024, $30$ problems; the six-benchmark
evaluation at the last step is what speaks to other questions, and its paired error
pools problems across seeds, which ignores clustering by seed.

\paragraph{Backbones.} The full design runs on one $1.5$B model. Two further backbones
carry the three-way contrast at one seed each, which establishes a sign and not a
size; they stop at $7$B, and the Llama row differs from the others in validation set
and in being instruction-tuned, each of which is why no accuracy is compared across
backbones.

\paragraph{The estimator.} STEER-V reads one realised continuation, which is unbiased
and noisy; the forecaster trades that variance for a signed bias that a calibration
must remove. Which error costs more inside a training loop is measured here at three
seeds (Section~\ref{sec:res:forecaster}), not settled in general, and a forecaster
whose error did not grow with $k$ would test a longer horizon.

\paragraph{The support is small by construction.} At most $\kappa(n-1)/T$ of positions
carry a non-zero $\AH$ --- on the order of a percent here. Whether the effect scales
with $n$, or with branch points chosen adaptively rather than at fixed depths, is
untested; and the widening of the spread across siblings, which is what a sum-zero
correction positively predicts, is not yet measured.

\paragraph{An assumption with no remedy.} Approximation B2 --- that a token's update
does not change the conditional distribution at other positions --- is false in a
network with shared parameters. It is inherited from the base method, but our
construction adds a second estimator that relies on it.

\paragraph{The damping strength.} All weighted arms use $\lambda_{\min} = 0.7$, the
value the base method's authors report; their released script uses $0.8$, which we
report as a follow-up. The value cancels in every contrast between two weighted arms,
including the two that carry the claim.

\paragraph{Changes to the registration.} Three changes were made to the registered
plan after it was first committed and before any run of this campaign: the training
length (to $150$ steps) and the compute-matched control, the replacement of one
refutation condition by the dissociation test, and the addition of the backbone rows.
Each is dated in the released registration.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work studies an optimization-time reweighting for reinforcement learning on
mathematics problems with automatically verifiable answers. It introduces no new data
collection, no human subjects, and no new model release. The intervention acts on
which of several equally-scored continuations receives a slightly smaller gradient
weight; it does not alter the reward, and so does not change what behavior is being
optimized for. The training data is a public competition-mathematics set and the
evaluation sets are public.

The main foreseeable risk is indirect. Methods that preserve diversity during RL
fine-tuning are not specific to mathematics, and the same mechanism would apply to a
reward encoding any objective, including a harmful one; the method inherits, and does
not mitigate, whatever the reward encodes. Its computational cost over the base
method is the tree sampler, which Section~\ref{sec:res:cost} quantifies.

\section{Implementation Notes}
\label{sec:appendix-impl}

\paragraph{Sibling mask.} For a group of $G$ rollouts of length $T$ we form
$\mathrm{eq}[i,j,t] = \mathbf{1}[y^{(i)}_t = y^{(j)}_t]$ and take a cumulative
product along $t$, so that $\mathrm{prefixeq}[i,j,t]$ is true exactly when the two
rollouts agree on $y_{\le t}$. Shifting it one position right gives agreement on
$y_{<t}$, which is the sibling relation Eq.~\eqref{eq:ah} requires. Padded positions
are treated as disagreement, so a length difference counts as a divergence.

\paragraph{The realised reward-to-go.} The trainer computes the policy's entropy at
every response position in the pass that produces the log-probabilities;
$\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}$ is the discounted sum of the next $\kappa$ of them,
zero past the end of a response, and needs no shift (Section~\ref{sec:approx}). The
forecaster variant materializes no head logits: each head's hidden states are
processed in chunks of $4096$ rows, mapped through the shared unembedding and reduced
to an entropy immediately, and its value is read at $t+1$ and moved to $t$.

\paragraph{LoRA in the tree sampler.} The tree calls the inference engine once per
stage, each with its own number of prompts; every call carries the current adapter,
and a run stops if the adapter is missing (which would silently sample from the base
model) or if more than one is registered.

\paragraph{Checkpoints and evaluation.} A run keeps steps $50$, $100$ and $150$ and
its best validation step; a superseded best is deleted only after the new checkpoint
is written. For evaluation the adapter is merged into the base model after checking
that every adapter tensor reached the model unchanged; an adapter whose $B$ matrices
are all zero is refused, since evaluating it would evaluate the base model.

\paragraph{Equivalence test.} A unit test asserts that with $\lambda = 0$ the token
weights are bitwise identical to the base method's on random inputs.

\section{Closed Forms for Section~\ref{sec:blind}}
\label{sec:appendix-closed}

Perturbing one logit of a $k$-way uniform distribution by $\delta$ and writing
$S := e^{\delta} + k - 1$ gives $\pi_1 = e^{\delta}/S$ and $\pi_j = 1/S$ for
$j \neq 1$, so
%
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
H(\delta) &= \log S - \frac{\delta e^{\delta}}{S},\\
H'(\delta) &= -\frac{(k-1)\,\delta\, e^{\delta}}{S^{2}},
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
%
whence $H'(0) = 0$ and $H''(0) = -(k-1)/k^{2}$. The sign of $H'$ is
$-\operatorname{sign}(\delta)$, so any perturbation lowers the entropy, but only at
second order. Table~\ref{tab:secondorder} contrasts this with a peaked distribution,
where the first-order term does not vanish.

\section{Pre-Registered Analysis}
\label{sec:appendix-prereg}

The analysis plan was committed to the artifact repository before any run of this
campaign; the file is \texttt{docs/preregistration\_lora.md} in the release, and what
follows summarizes it.

\paragraph{Setting.} As in Section~\ref{sec:setup}: one base model, the same data,
LoRA of rank $64$ at learning rate $1\mathrm{e}{-5}$ for every arm, $150$ steps, token
weights in $[0.7, 1]$, three seeds for the six core arms and one for the follow-ups
and backbones.

\paragraph{Endpoints.} Primary: six-benchmark accuracy at $32$ samples per problem at
the last step, read by direction consistency (four or more of six support, three
equivocal, two or fewer against) and by the paired across-problem difference.
Secondary: the same at the AIME24-best checkpoint, and AIME24 over the converged
window of steps $40$--$150$, paired within seed.

\paragraph{Refutation conditions.} The four of Section~\ref{sec:endpoints}, stated in
advance so they cannot be renegotiated afterwards.

\paragraph{The estimator.} The realised-value method was chosen for cost and for
removing the forecaster's warm-up and calibration. In the one pilot that compared the
two estimators at a single seed, the forecast led; so the forecaster runs at every
seed, and its contrast is read by a rule fixed in advance: within two standard errors
the two are reported as not distinguished, not as equivalent.

\paragraph{Collapse under LoRA.} GRPO's entropy at step $10$ and at the last step, per
seed; collapse is the last value below half the first in every seed. If GRPO does not
collapse, the paper says so and does not motivate the method by collapse under LoRA.

\paragraph{Changes before the first run.} Recorded with dates in the file: the training
length and the compute-matched control; one refutation condition, first copied from
the plan of an earlier campaign where its own manuscript had retired it as the wrong
instrument, replaced by the dissociation test; and the backbone rows.
