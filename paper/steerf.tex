\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) drives a policy's entropy
toward zero, and a recent family of methods counters this by re-weighting the policy
loss with a per-token estimate of each update's entropy cost. These methods account
for how visited states sharpen, but not for how an update changes \emph{which states
are visited}. We show that the gradient of the trajectory entropy splits exactly into
a \emph{local} channel --- the change of the conditional distribution at visited
states --- and a \emph{visitation} channel --- the change in the probability of
reaching them --- and that token-level estimators capture only the first. The local
estimate vanishes, correctly to first order, at a flat branch point, which is exactly
where the visitation prefactor is largest, so no refinement of the local estimator
recovers the missing term. We derive the visitation term as a per-token REINFORCE
advantage $\AH$ whose reward-to-go is the remaining conditional entropy, made
estimable by a sibling-prefix baseline that a fixed tree-structured rollout supplies.
$\AH$ sums to zero within each sibling set, so it can change trajectory diversity
without changing aggregate token entropy. A five-arm experiment over three paired
seeds observes this dissociation: changing the damping mechanism shifts the aggregate
entropy by $\num{Csteergrpoentropy}$ and $\num{Cuniformsteerentropy}$, whereas changing
only the branch-specific $\AH$ signal shifts it by $\num{Csigneduniformentropy}$ and
$\num{Csignedpermutedentropy}$. We do not establish an accuracy gain from the
correction --- a pre-registered accuracy test is not supported --- and the experiments
instead support the structural claim that aggregate entropy cannot diagnose the
visitation channel.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Policy-gradient fine-tuning with verifiable rewards sharpens a language model's
output distribution. Measured as the mean per-token entropy, the sharpening is fast,
monotone and, past a point, coincident with the end of useful learning. The standard
remedies spend part of the update budget slowing it down: an entropy bonus, a KL
anchor, a clip on the tokens that lose most entropy, or, most recently, a per-token
re-weighting of the policy loss by a first-order estimate of the entropy each token's
update destroys \citep{steer}.

These remedies account for how the conditional distribution at a visited state
sharpens. They do not account for how an update changes which states are visited, and
this omission is structural rather than a matter of estimator quality.
Figure~\ref{fig:branch} shows the case that makes it concrete. At a state with two
equiprobable continuations the conditional distribution is as entropic as it can be,
and its first-order entropy change under a small push is zero. Yet a small shift of
mass from a continuation that leads into a rich sub-tree to one that leads into a
dead end removes all the downstream diversity the first carried. The branch itself
is locally safe; which branch is visited is not.

We make this precise. Write $\Htraj(\pit \mid x)$ for the entropy of the distribution
over whole responses. Its gradient splits, by the product rule and with no
approximation, into a \emph{local} term that holds visited states fixed --- what a
per-token estimate measures --- and a \emph{visitation} term that holds per-state
entropies fixed and differentiates the probability of reaching each state. The local
estimate is proportional to the deviation of the sampled token's surprisal from its
mean; it vanishes at a flat branch point, correctly, since the entropy loss there is
second order. The visitation term's prefactor $1 - \pit(y_t \mid s_t)$ is largest at
those same points.

The missing term is estimable. Causality turns it into a REINFORCE estimator whose
reward-to-go is the remaining conditional entropy $\Htogo$, and a baseline from
rollouts that share a prefix turns that into a per-token advantage $\AH$. Plain group
rollouts almost never share prefixes, so we schedule the sharing with a fixed tree,
which we use only as a device for the estimator. Because $\AH$ is a deviation from a
sibling mean it sums to zero over each sibling set: it changes which sibling is damped,
not how much damping there is on average. This gives the experiment its target. The
aggregate entropy curve --- the evidence usually offered for entropy control --- should
move when the damping mechanism changes and should not move when only the
branch-specific $\AH$ signal changes, a prediction that can fail.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{An exact decomposition} of the trajectory-entropy gradient into a local
      term, which changes conditional entropy at visited states, and a visitation term,
      which changes which states the policy reaches (Section~\ref{sec:channels}).
\item \textbf{A structural mismatch:} the local estimator used by current token-level
      methods vanishes at flat branch points, precisely where the visitation prefactor
      is largest, so the omission cannot be repaired within the local channel
      (Section~\ref{sec:blind}).
\item \textbf{An estimable visitation term:} a per-token REINFORCE advantage $\AH$ whose
      reward-to-go is the remaining conditional entropy, with a sibling-prefix baseline,
      a ledger of every approximation and the sign of its error, and a bound on how many
      positions can carry it under any sampler (Section~\ref{sec:ahdef}).
\item \textbf{A diagnostic consequence:} because the correction is mean-preserving within
      sibling sets, aggregate token entropy is not an appropriate diagnostic for whether
      it acts (Section~\ref{sec:zerosum}).
\item \textbf{An experiment} with five arms that separates changes to the damping
      mechanism from changes to the branch-specific signal and observes the predicted
      dissociation in aggregate entropy (Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:res:entropy}).
\end{itemize}
We do not establish an accuracy gain from the correction: the pre-registered test of
the $\AH$ signal against a signal-free control is not supported
(Section~\ref{sec:res:contrasts}).

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Notation.} A prompt is $x$, a response $y = y_{1:T}$, and $s_t = (x,
y_{<t})$ the state before token $t$. $H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$ is a per-position
conditional entropy and $\Htraj(\pit \mid x)$ the entropy over whole responses. The
surprisal of a token is $I_a := -\log \pit(a \mid s_t)$, so $H = \E_{a}[I_a]$. We
optimize with GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}, which samples $n$ responses per prompt
and broadcasts the group-standardized reward to every token.

\paragraph{Token-level entropy control.} \citet{steer} leave the advantage untouched
and multiply each token's clipped surrogate by a weight $\alpha_t \in
[\lambda_{\min}, 1]$ derived from the first-order change in the conditional entropy
at a \emph{fixed} state after one step,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Om(s) \propto -\,\E_{a}\Bigl[&\tfrac{\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{clip}} A}{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}}\,
  \pit(a)\bigl(1-\pit(a)\bigr)\\
  &\cdot\bigl(\log \pit(a) + H\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
evaluated at the sampled token and mapped into the band by $\alpha_u =
\exp(-\alpha\,|\Om_u| / \max_v |\Om_v|)$, $\lambda_{\min} = e^{-\alpha}$, so the
weight can only attenuate.

\paragraph{Entropy collapse in RLVR.} Entropy decay under policy-gradient fine-tuning
of reasoning models is well documented and tied to the stall of improvement
\citep{cui2025bentropy}; it is countered by an asymmetric clip
\citep{yu2025dapo}, by restricting updates on high-covariance tokens
\citep{cui2025bentropy}, or by KL control with reference resets over long training
\citep{liu2025bprorl}.

\paragraph{Entropy regularization.} Maximum-entropy reinforcement learning adds the
policy entropy to the objective \citep{ziebart2008maximum,haarnoja2018soft}. In RLVR a
naive entropy bonus has been reported not to prevent collapse and to degrade accuracy
\citep{rethinkingentropyreg}, and maximum entropy can mislead policy optimization
\citep{zhang2025cwhen}. Both bonus and KL act on conditional distributions at visited
states, that is, on the local channel.

\paragraph{Token-level intervention.} \citet{wang2025dbeyond} show that training on a
minority of high-entropy ``forking'' tokens suffices, which motivates per-token
treatment but selects tokens by local entropy. Other schemes shape advantages or
rewards with token entropy \citep{cheng2025reasoning,tan2025gtpo,zhang2025dedge}.
\citet{steer} estimate each token's first-order effect on conditional entropy, the
estimator our analysis extends.

\paragraph{Trajectory-level diversity.} Token-level entropy can stay high while the
diversity of final answers falls \citep{wu2025ainvisible}, and explicit outcome-level
exploration targets that diversity directly \citep{song2025outcome}. The visitation
channel is a mechanism for the first observation: per-state entropies can be
preserved while the trajectory distribution concentrates.

Prior work either controls conditional entropy at visited states or selects tokens by
local uncertainty. We instead differentiate the entropy of the trajectory distribution
itself, which reveals a term caused by changes in which states the updated policy
reaches. We use Medusa-style heads \citep{medusa} not for decoding but as an entropy
forecaster. Appendix~\ref{sec:related} expands this section.

\section{Two Channels}
\label{sec:channels}

The autoregressive factorization and the tower rule give the identity
\begin{equation}
\Htraj(\pit \mid x) = \E_{y \sim \pit}\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_{t} H\bigl(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t)\bigr)\Bigr].
\label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
Both the integrand and the measure $\E_{y\sim\pit}$ depend on $\theta$. Writing $f_\theta(y)
:= \sum_t H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t(y)))$, the product rule and the score-function
identity give, with no approximation,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\gradt \Htraj
&= \underbrace{\E\bigl[\gradt f_\theta(y)\bigr]}_{(\mathrm{L})\ \text{local}}\\
&\quad + \underbrace{\E\bigl[f_\theta(y)\,\gradt \log \pit(y \mid x)\bigr]}_{(\mathrm{V})\ \text{visitation}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:twochannel}
\end{equation}
(L) holds the visited states fixed; Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} is a first-order estimate
of it. (V) holds per-state entropies fixed and asks how the probability of reaching
each state changes; $\Om$ reads the distribution at a position and never the
probability of arriving there. The two can have opposite signs, so driving (L) to
zero does not bound $\gradt \Htraj$. Appendix~\ref{sec:app:derivation} gives every
step.

\subsection{Why the local channel cannot be repaired}
\label{sec:blind}

One might expect the channels to coincide, since branch points are uncertain
positions; in warm-up rollouts branch diversity does correlate with local entropy
($\rho = 0.65$). The inference fails in three steps.

\emph{(i) $\Om$ reads a deviation, not a level.} Collecting constants,
$\Om \propto c\,\pi_a(1-\pi_a)\,(H - I_a)$, and $H = \E[I_a]$, so the last factor
has mean zero: $|\Om|$ is set by the \emph{spread} of surprisal, not the height of
the entropy. \emph{(ii) At a uniform branch point $\Om \equiv 0$}, since $I_a = \log
k = H$ for every $a$. \emph{(iii) That zero is correct.} The logit derivative of the
conditional entropy \citep[Eq.~20]{steer},
$\partial H / \partial z_a = -\pit(a)(\log \pit(a) + H)$, vanishes for every $a$ at
a uniform distribution; perturbing one logit by $\delta$ gives
$H''(0) = -(k-1)/k^2$, so the local loss is entirely second order. Uniform branch
points are locally safe, and an estimator that allocates them no protection is
correct for its own objective.

The visitation term's prefactor, derived in Appendix~\ref{sec:weights}, is
$1 - \pi_a$, which tends to $1$ as the distribution flattens --- where a group of
samples is also certain to diverge. Table~\ref{tab:anticorr} evaluates both on a
family of distributions from confident to flat: $|\Om|$ is largest where the model is
confident but a tail token was sampled, and is zero where divergence is certain and
the visitation prefactor is maximal. The local signal vanishes precisely in the
regime where the visitation prefactor is largest, so no refinement of the local term
recovers what it omits.

\section{Estimating the Visitation Channel}
\label{sec:ahdef}

\paragraph{A reward-to-go.} Expanding (V) into a double sum over the position $t$ of
an entropy and the position $u$ of a score, every term with $u \ge t$ is exactly zero:
$H(\pit(\cdot\mid s_t))$ is measurable given $y_{<u}$ and the expected score at a
position vanishes. Only $u < t$ survives, and
\begin{equation}
(\mathrm{V}) = \E\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_u \gradt \log \pit(y_u \mid s_u)\,
\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u)\Bigr],
\label{eq:togo}
\end{equation}
with $\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) := \sum_{t > u} H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$. $\Htogo$ was
not posited: it is the reward-to-go of this REINFORCE estimator. Any function of
$s_u$ alone is an admissible baseline, giving
\begin{equation}
\AH(s_u, y_u) := \Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) - \bar{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}(s_u),
\label{eq:ah}
\end{equation}
and up to here no approximation has been made. Our baseline is the mean of $\Htogo$
over rollouts in the same group whose prefixes $y_{<u}$ agree exactly; with no
sibling it equals the rollout's own value and $\AH = 0$.

\paragraph{Siblings, and a ceiling.} The forecast below is a deterministic function
of the causal prefix, so
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\AH[i, u] \neq 0 \iff\ &\text{siblings share } y_{<u}\\
&\text{but diverge at } y_u .
\end{split}
\label{eq:branchonly}
\end{equation}
Independent samples from a sharpened policy diverge at their first token and never
share a prefix again, so the visitation channel is identically absent. We therefore
schedule the sharing: a tree sampler cuts a root at fixed depths with branching
factor $2$, yielding $n = 8$ leaves. The depths are fixed rather than chosen, and we
make no claim for the schedule as a sampler. Since $n$ sequences form a trie with at
most $n - 1$ branching nodes, the fraction of positions with $\AH \ne 0$ is at most
$2(n-1)/T$ under \emph{any} sampler --- about one percent at $T \approx 10^3$.

\paragraph{The correction cannot move the mean.}
\label{sec:zerosum}
On each sibling set $\sum_j \AH(s_u, y^{(j)}_u) = 0$. A correction built on $\AH$
therefore cannot change the average weight at branch tokens, only spread siblings
apart; this is forced by unbiasedness, not a defect. ``Mean entropy at branch points
rose'' is \emph{not} a prediction of this method, and an average over all response
tokens should barely register it: aggregate token entropy is not a diagnostic for
whether the correction acts. The prediction it does make is that the spread
\emph{across siblings} widens. A hypothesis that raises the mean is different and
weaker, and we run it as a control.

\paragraph{From $\AH$ to a token weight.} $\Htogo$ involves states the update has not
visited, so we forecast it from the hidden state with $\kappa = 2$ parallel
multi-token-prediction heads, discounted by $\gamma_H$ and calibrated per head:
\begin{equation}
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}} = \max\Bigl(0, \textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa} \gamma_H^{\,k}\bigl(a_k H_k(h_u) + b_k\bigr)\Bigr).
\label{eq:forecast}
\end{equation}
The head substitution has a known sign: a head models the marginal of a token $k$
steps ahead, whose entropy exceeds the conditional one by a mutual information that
grows with $k$, so the forecast always over-estimates and the affine calibration
absorbs it (fitted $a_1 = 1.03$, $a_{k\ge2} \le 0.19$). Under the base method's
Assumption~\ref{ass:indep}, one step moves the sampled token's log-probability by
$\eta w_u (1 - \pi_u)$, where $w_u$ is the clipped advantage coefficient, giving the
visitation prediction $\mathrm{visit}_u = \eta w_u (1-\pi_u)\,\mathrm{clip}(\AH,
\pm c)$. When mass moves toward a branch with $\AH > 0$ trajectory entropy rises; when
it moves toward one with $\AH < 0$, into a dead end, the abandoned siblings' diversity
is destroyed --- the collapse path (L) cannot see. Because $\Om$ is heavy-tailed, we
add the visitation term \emph{after} the base map, as a bounded adjustment on the
support $\mathcal{S} = \{u : \mathrm{visit}_u \ne 0\}$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha^{\mathrm{f}}_u = \mathrm{clamp}\bigl(\alpha_u
  + \lambda\, b\, \tanh(\mathrm{visit}_u / \rho),\ \lambda_{\min}, 1\bigr),
\label{eq:combine}
\end{equation}
with $b$ the band width and $\rho$ the RMS of $\mathrm{visit}$ over $\mathcal{S}$. The
$\tanh$ preserves the sign and caps one token's movement at $\lambda b$; at
$\lambda = 0$ the pipeline is bitwise the base method. Table~\ref{tab:ledger} lists
every approximation and Appendix~\ref{sec:approx} derives each. The strongest are A2,
whose error has a known sign and monotonicity, and B2, which is false for a network
with shared parameters and is inherited from the base method.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Training.} All runs fine-tune Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B \citep{yang2024aqwen} on
DAPO-Math-17k \citep{yu2025dapo} for $110$ steps of $512$ prompts with GRPO, $n = 8$,
constant learning rate $1\mathrm{e}{-6}$, responses up to $3072$ tokens, no entropy
bonus or KL loss, and an asymmetric clip ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.2$,
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.28$). The base method's map uses $\lambda_{\min} =
0.7$, its paper's value \citep[App.~F.1]{steer}. STEER-F uses $\kappa = 2$, $\gamma_H =
0.7$, $\lambda = 0.25$, $c = 1$, and a tree cut at depths $\{64, 192, 384\}$. Every arm
shares the data seed per run and the initial checkpoint. Appendix~\ref{sec:setup-app}
lists the rest.

\paragraph{Five arms.} We call everything except the content of $\AH$ --- the tree
sampler, the support $\mathcal{S}$ and the token-weight damping --- the \emph{damping
mechanism}. Comparing the base method with the full method changes the mechanism and
the signal at once, so we add two arms that keep the whole mechanism and remove only
the branch-specific $\AH$ signal (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). \textsc{uniform} applies the
same attenuation to every position in the support: it removes the $\AH$ signal while
preserving its support, which implements the ``raise the mean'' hypothesis.
\textsc{permuted} shuffles $\AH$ within each sibling set: it preserves the distribution
of $\AH$ magnitudes and the zero sum but destroys the branch--score assignment.

every $10$ steps; one evaluation has an across-problem standard error of

$40$--$110$. The seed is the unit of analysis: each run is collapsed to its window
mean, arms are paired within a seed, and the test is a paired $t$ with
$\mathrm{df} = n - 1$, over the $\num{Nseedsused}$ seeds on which all five arms
finished ($\num{Seedset}$). We report acc@32, maj@32 and their difference (uplift).
The logged entropy is a token mean, i.e.\ $\Htraj$ divided by mean length. The
analysis plan was registered before the runs (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}), and
every figure is recomputed from the training logs by one script.

\paragraph{Question.} Does aggregate entropy respond to the $\AH$ signal itself? The
construction says it should not: $\AH$ is non-zero on at most $2(n-1)/T$ of positions
and redistributes weight among siblings without adding any
(Section~\ref{sec:zerosum}). Both statements were in the manuscript before the
campaign was launched.

\paragraph{Result.} Across all three paired seeds, changing the damping mechanism
produces substantially larger shifts in aggregate entropy than changing the $\AH$
signal while holding the mechanism fixed (Table~\ref{tab:entropy}). Turning the
damping on shifts it by $\num{Csteergrpoentropy}$, and adding the tree with uniform
damping by $\num{Cuniformsteerentropy}$; removing the $\AH$ signal shifts it by
$\num{Csigneduniformentropy}$, and destroying the branch--score assignment by
$\num{Csignedpermutedentropy}$. The smaller mechanism effect is more than twice the
larger signal effect. Accuracy runs the other way: the signal contrasts give
$\num{Csigneduniformacc}$ and $\num{Csignedpermutedacc}$, the mechanism contrasts
$\num{Csteergrpoacc}$ and $\num{Cuniformsteeracc}$.

(Appendix~\ref{sec:res:diag}); a registered condition on the ordering of arm means was
retired rather than scored, for reasons given in Appendix~\ref{sec:res:entropy-app}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Current token-level entropy control in RLVR accounts for how visited states sharpen,
but not for how policy updates change which states are visited. The gradient of
trajectory entropy has both terms; the local estimator vanishes, for a correct reason,
exactly where the visitation term is largest, so the omission cannot be repaired by a
better local estimator. The missing term is a REINFORCE estimator whose reward-to-go
is the remaining conditional entropy, and a fixed tree supplies the sibling baseline
that makes it estimable. The resulting correction is mean-preserving, so it can change
trajectory diversity without a corresponding change in aggregate token entropy, and the
experiment observes that dissociation: aggregate entropy tracks the damping mechanism,
not the $\AH$ signal. We do not establish an accuracy benefit, and this paper should be
read as an account of where token-level entropy control acts and of what an aggregate
entropy curve cannot see, not as a recommendation to adopt the method for accuracy.
Whether a cheaper estimator of $\Htogo$ suffices, and whether structured sampling helps
RLVR in its own right, remain open.


\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Empirical scope.} Every result is measured on one $1.5$B
mathematics-specialized model, on $\num{Nseedsused}$ paired seeds
($\num{Seedset}$; the registration planned five, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}),
and validated on AIME24's $30$ problems, where one evaluation carries an
across-problem standard error of $\num{SEaime}$. The seed count bounds effect sizes
rather than the structural result, which is a comparison between groups of contrasts;
but the accuracy gaps are inside the spread across seeds --- GRPO's converged accuracy
moves by $\num{Sgrpoacc}$ across its seeds --- and the pre-registered accuracy test of
the $\AH$ signal is not supported (Section~\ref{sec:res:contrasts}). The six-benchmark
check covers one seed per arm. The base method lands below GRPO in our runs, where its
paper reports it above; we ran it at its paper's $\lambda_{\min} = 0.7$ rather than its
launcher's $0.8$, on $110$ rather than $200$ steps, and cannot separate these causes
(Appendix~\ref{sec:res:ablate}). This reaches STEER-F $-$ \textsc{steer} and
\textsc{steer} $-$ GRPO, not the contrasts against \textsc{uniform} and
\textsc{permuted}, which hold every setting fixed. The comparison with published
numbers (Section~\ref{sec:res:published}) is uncontrolled: the quoted rows were not
re-run, our AIME24 column is the selection value and biased upward, and the sampler in
those runs was chosen after the campaign.

\paragraph{Estimator assumptions and efficiency.} The calibration suppresses heads
beyond the first, so $\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}$ is close to a one-step forecast, and on a
frozen policy the policy's own next-token entropy reproduces $\AH$ at
$R^2 = \num{Locrsq}$; the heads carry the step-time overhead of
Appendix~\ref{sec:res:cost}. The in-loop \textsc{oracle} comparison favours the heads
by $\num{Fsignedoracleacc}$ at one seed, which orders the two without sizing the
difference; whether the heads earn their cost is open. Approximation B2, that an
update does not change the conditional distribution at other positions, is false for
a network with shared parameters; it is inherited from the base method, but our
construction adds a second estimator that rests on it. The support is small by
construction --- at most $2(n-1)/T$, about one position in eighty here.

\paragraph{Open structural predictions.} The prefactor half of
Section~\ref{sec:blind} is analytic, but we have not measured the local and visitation
terms together at real sibling-divergence positions in live rollouts, so
Table~\ref{tab:anticorr} is a prediction rather than an observation. The positive
prediction of the zero sum --- that the spread across siblings widens --- is
instrumented but not carried by any run reported here; the dissociation we measure is
its negative half. Whether the effect scales with the number of samples or with an
adaptively chosen branching schedule is untested, and whether structured rollout
sampling improves RLVR in its own right is a question this paper raises and does not
answer.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work studies an optimization-time regularizer for reinforcement learning on
mathematics problems with automatically verifiable answers. It introduces no new
data collection, no human subjects, and no new model release. The intervention acts
on which of several equally-scored continuations receives a slightly smaller
gradient weight; it does not alter the reward, and so does not change what behavior
is being optimized for. The training data is a public competition-mathematics set
and the evaluation set is a public examination.

The main foreseeable risk is indirect. Methods that preserve diversity during RL
fine-tuning are not specific to mathematics, and the same mechanism would apply to a
reward model encoding any objective, including a harmful one; we note that our
method inherits, and does not mitigate, whatever the reward function encodes. The
computational cost of the method (a forward pass through a set of small prediction
heads at every optimization step) is a real environmental cost, which we quantify in
Section~\ref{sec:res:cost} so that it can be weighed against the benefit.

\section{Extended Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Entropy collapse in RLVR.}
Entropy decay under policy-gradient fine-tuning is well documented
\citep{cui2025bentropy}, and a range of interventions add an explicit entropy term
to the objective, in the tradition of maximum-entropy reinforcement learning
\citep{ziebart2008maximum,haarnoja2018soft}, or constrain the update so that
high-entropy positions are preserved. Recent analyses report that naive
entropy regularization does not prevent the collapse and that continuing to train
under it degrades accuracy \citep{rethinkingentropyreg}, which is consistent with
our reading: the regularizer acts on the local channel, and the local channel is
not where trajectory diversity is being lost.

\paragraph{Assumption.}
Both \citet{steer} and this work rely on one structural assumption, which we restate
so that our approximation ledger can refer to it.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Assumption 1} (parameter-independent softmax; \citealp{steer}).
\label{ass:indep}
For any state $s$, each action $a$ carries an independent logit $z_{s,a}(\theta)$, so
that a gradient step on the sampled token does not substantially move the logits of
the other tokens at that state.
\end{quote}

\noindent
It is false for a network with shared parameters. We inherit it rather than
introduce it, but our construction adds a second estimator that rests on it.

\paragraph{Token-level entropy control.}
The method we build on re-weights the clipped surrogate loss by a per-token
first-order estimate of the entropy change that token's update induces
\citep{steer}. That work observes that earlier interventions are ``indirect'' and
that their effect is therefore limited and can fail. Our decomposition supplies a
mechanism for that observation: the estimate is a first-order estimate of the local
channel only, and it is exactly zero at the states where the visitation channel is
largest.

\paragraph{Which tokens carry the gradient.}
\citet{wang2025dbeyond} show that restricting policy-gradient updates to a small
minority of high-entropy ``forking'' tokens matches full-gradient training. That
result establishes that the identity of the tokens, not the aggregate entropy,
is what matters, and it motivates a per-token treatment; it does not, however,
distinguish the two channels, since forking tokens are identified by their local
entropy. Our analysis predicts that a local criterion will select a systematically
different set from the one the visitation channel would select.

\paragraph{Local stochasticity without global diversity.}
\citet{wu2025ainvisible} report that token-level entropy can remain high while the
diversity of final answers falls. This is precisely the signature our decomposition
predicts when the visitation channel is unmanaged: per-state entropies are
preserved --- the local channel is doing its job --- while the trajectory
distribution concentrates.

\paragraph{Multi-token prediction.}
We use Medusa-style parallel prediction heads \citep{medusa} not for their intended
purpose (speculative decoding) but as an entropy forecaster: we need the entropy of
the distribution over a token $k$ steps ahead, not a good sample of that token.
Section~\ref{sec:approx} shows that this choice of estimator has a signed,
monotone error which we characterize and correct.

\subsection{Preliminaries}

\paragraph{Notation.}
A prompt is $x$, a response is $y = y_{1:T}$, and the state before emitting token
$t$ is $s_t = (x, y_{<t})$. We write $H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$ for the entropy of
the conditional distribution at a single position (lowercase, per-token) and
$\Htraj(\pit \mid x)$ for the entropy of the distribution over whole responses
(uppercase, per-trajectory). The surprisal of a token $a$ is
$I_a := -\log \pit(a \mid s_t)$, so that $H = \E_{a \sim \pit}[I_a]$.

\paragraph{Group-relative policy optimization.}
We optimize with GRPO. For each prompt, $n$ responses are sampled and scored, and
each response receives the group-standardized advantage
$A_i = (r_i - \mathrm{mean}_j r_j) / \mathrm{std}_j r_j$, broadcast to every token
of that response. Replacing a learned value function with the group mean is what
makes GRPO cheaper than PPO; it also means that a group whose members all receive
the same reward contributes no gradient.

\paragraph{Token-level entropy control.}
\citet{steer} leave the advantage untouched and instead multiply each token's
contribution to the clipped surrogate by a weight
$\alpha_t \in [\lambda_{\min}, 1]$ derived from a first-order estimate of the
entropy change that token's update induces. Their Theorem~1 gives that estimate in
closed form: under a parameter-independent softmax
(their Assumption~1; our~\ref{ass:indep}), the change in the conditional entropy at
a \emph{fixed} state $s$ after one GRPO step is, to first order,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Om(s) = -\frac{\eta}{L}\, \E_{a}\Bigl[
  &\frac{\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{clip}}(s,a)\, A(s,a)}{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(a \mid s)}\\
  &\cdot\, \pit(a \mid s)\bigl(1 - \pit(a \mid s)\bigr)\\
  &\cdot\, \bigl(\log \pit(a \mid s) + H\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:omega-app}
\end{equation}
%
where $\eta$ is the learning rate, $L$ the total decoded length in the update, and
$\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{clip}}$ the PPO clipping indicator. The implementation
evaluates the bracket at the sampled token rather than in expectation. The policy
loss becomes $\sum_u \alpha_u \cdot \mathrm{PPO\text{-}clip}(A_u, r_u)$, with
%
\begin{equation}
\alpha_u \;=\; \exp\!\Bigl(-\alpha\, \frac{|\Om_u|}{\max_v |\Om_v|}\Bigr),
\qquad \lambda_{\min} = e^{-\alpha},
\label{eq:steermap}
\end{equation}
%
a batch-normalized exponential map into $[\lambda_{\min}, 1]$, so the weight can
only attenuate. The factor $1/\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} will
matter later: it makes $\Om$ heavy-tailed, which is why the two channels must be
normalized before they are combined (Section~\ref{sec:combine}).

\subsection{Two Channels}
\label{sec:channels-app}

\subsubsection{Step 1: the chain rule}

The object we would like to protect is the entropy of the distribution the policy
induces over \emph{whole responses},
%
\begin{equation}
\Htraj(\pit \mid x) := -\sum_{y} \pit(y \mid x)\, \log \pit(y \mid x),
\label{eq:Hdef}
\end{equation}
%
a sum over an exponentially large set. The autoregressive factorization
$\pit(y \mid x) = \prod_t \pit(y_t \mid s_t)$ turns the logarithm into a sum and
lets us exchange the order of summation:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Htraj
&= -\sum_{y} \pit(y \mid x) \sum_{t=1}^{T} \log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\\
&= \sum_{t=1}^{T} \E_{y \sim \pit}\bigl[-\log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\bigr].
\end{split}
\label{eq:chainstep}
\end{equation}
%
Each summand is still an expectation over whole responses, but the integrand depends
on $y$ only through $y_{\le t}$. Conditioning on the prefix and applying the tower
rule replaces the inner expectation with a per-position entropy,
%
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
\E_{y}&\bigl[-\log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\bigr]\\
&= \E_{y_{<t}}\Bigl[\,\underbrace{\E_{y_t \sim \pit(\cdot \mid s_t)}\bigl[-\log \pit(y_t \mid s_t)\bigr]}_{= \; H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))}\Bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
%
because the inner expectation is, by definition, the entropy of the conditional
distribution at $s_t$. Collecting the terms back under a single expectation gives
%
\begin{equation}
\Htraj(\pit \mid x)
= \E_{y \sim \pit}\Bigl[\sum_{t=1}^{T} H\bigl(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t)\bigr)\Bigr].
\label{eq:chain-app}
\end{equation}
%
Equation~\eqref{eq:chain} is an identity, not an approximation. Its left-hand side
is what we want to protect; its right-hand side is what instrumentation can measure,
since $H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$ is read directly off the logits at each position.

The entire argument of this paper follows from one feature of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain} that is easy to pass over: \emph{the measure
$\E_{y \sim \pit}$ depends on $\theta$ as well}. The right-hand side is not a sum of
$\theta$-dependent terms over a fixed set of positions --- it is a $\theta$-dependent
average of them over a $\theta$-dependent set of trajectories.

\subsubsection{Step 2: the product rule}

Write the per-trajectory total
%
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(y) := \sum_{t=1}^{T} H\bigl(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t(y))\bigr),
\label{eq:fdef}
\end{equation}
%
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain} reads
$\Htraj = \sum_y \pit(y \mid x)\, f_\theta(y)$. Both factors of every summand carry
$\theta$, so differentiating requires the product rule:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\gradt \Htraj
&= \sum_y \bigl[\gradt \pit(y \mid x)\bigr] f_\theta(y)\\
&\quad + \sum_y \pit(y \mid x)\, \gradt f_\theta(y).
\end{split}
\label{eq:productrule}
\end{equation}
%
The second sum is already an expectation. The first is not, because
$\gradt \pit$ is not a probability distribution; the score-function identity
$\gradt \pit = \pit \gradt \log \pit$ restores it to one. Substituting and naming
the two terms,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\gradt \Htraj
&= \underbrace{\E\bigl[\gradt f_\theta(y)\bigr]}_{(\mathrm{L})\ \text{local}}\\
&\quad + \underbrace{\E\bigl[f_\theta(y)\,\gradt \log \pit(y \mid x)\bigr]}_{(\mathrm{V})\ \text{visitation}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:twochannel-app}
\end{equation}
%
Equations~\eqref{eq:Hdef}--\eqref{eq:twochannel} contain no approximation of any
kind. The split into two channels is forced by the product rule, not chosen.

\subsubsection{Reading the two terms}

\textbf{(L), the local channel}, holds the visited states fixed and asks how the
conditional distribution at each one changes. Equation~\eqref{eq:omega} is a
first-order estimate of exactly this.

\textbf{(V), the visitation channel}, holds the per-state entropies fixed and asks
how the probability of reaching each state changes. An update that concentrates mass
on one continuation makes the sub-tree below it more likely and the sub-trees below
its siblings less likely. Nothing in Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} can see this: $\Om$ reads
the conditional distribution at a position and never reads the probability of
arriving there.

The two terms can have opposite signs. A method that drives (L) to zero has not
thereby bounded $\gradt \Htraj$.

\subsubsection{Why the local channel cannot be repaired}
\label{sec:blind-app}

A natural objection is that the two channels might coincide in practice: branch
points are positions where the policy is uncertain, so they are high-entropy
positions, so $\Om$ should be large there and the local term should protect them
automatically. The premise is correct --- in warm-up rollouts on our model, branch
diversity correlates with local entropy at Spearman $\rho = 0.65$, and with the
entropy-to-go at $\rho = 0.82$. The inference from it fails, in three steps.

\paragraph{Step 1: $\Om$ reads a deviation, not a level.}
Collecting the constants of Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} into
$c := \eta\,\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{clip}} A / (L\,\pi_{\mathrm{old}})$ and rewriting in
terms of surprisal gives, for the contribution of a single action $a$,
%
\begin{equation}
\Om \;\propto\; c\;\pi_a(1-\pi_a)\,\bigl(H - I_a\bigr),
\label{eq:omega-surprisal}
\end{equation}
%
and $H = \E_{a\sim\pi}[I_a]$ by definition. The factor $H - I_a$ is therefore a
mean-zero fluctuation: what sets the magnitude of $|\Om|$ is the \emph{spread} of
surprisal, not the \emph{height} of the entropy. Nothing in $c$ depends on the shape
of $\pi(\cdot \mid s)$, so the argument below is unaffected by the clipping
indicator or the importance weight.

\paragraph{Step 2: at a uniform branch point $\Om \equiv 0$.}
If the distribution is uniform over $k$ candidates then $I_a = \log k = H$ for every
$a$, so $\Om$ vanishes identically. The positions that are most branch-like --- several
continuations at comparable probability --- are exactly where the local term
contributes nothing.

\paragraph{Step 3: and that zero is the correct answer.}
This is the step that closes the argument, and it follows from
\citeauthor{steer}'s own derivation. Differentiating the conditional entropy with
respect to a single logit gives \citep[Eq.~20]{steer}
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\frac{\partial H(\pit \mid s)}{\partial z_{s,a}}
= -\,\pit(a \mid s)\bigl(&\log \pit(a \mid s)\\[-2pt]
  &+ H(\pit \mid s)\bigr),
\end{split}
\label{eq:dHdz}
\end{equation}
%
and at a uniform distribution $\log \pit(a \mid s) = -\log k = -H$ makes the bracket
vanish for \emph{every} $a$. So $\partial H / \partial z_a = 0$ identically: $\Om$
is not wrong, it is right. Perturbing one logit of a uniform distribution by $\delta$ and writing
$S := e^{\delta} + k - 1$ gives the closed form
%
\begin{equation}
H(\delta) = \log S - \frac{\delta e^{\delta}}{S},
\qquad
H''(0) = -\,\frac{k-1}{k^{2}},
\label{eq:secondorder}
\end{equation}
%
with $H'(\delta)$ of sign $-\operatorname{sign}(\delta)$: any push in any direction
lowers the entropy, but the loss is entirely second order. For a $10$-way uniform
distribution and $\delta = 0.1$ the actual entropy loss is $4.7\times10^{-4}$,
roughly two orders of magnitude below that of a peaked distribution under the same
perturbation. \emph{As far as local entropy is concerned, uniform branch points are
genuinely safe}, and a local estimator that allocates them no protection is
allocating correctly for its own objective. No refinement of the local term
recovers the missing signal, because the missing signal is not local.

\paragraph{The two channels are oppositely ordered.}
The visitation term's prefactor, derived in Section~\ref{sec:weights}, is
$1 - \pi_a$, which approaches $1$ as the distribution flattens --- and the
probability that a group of $n$ samples actually diverges at a position approaches
$1$ in the same limit. Table~\ref{tab:anticorr} evaluates both channels on a family
of distributions that interpolates between confident and flat. $|\Om|$ is dominated
by positions where the model is fairly confident but a tail token was sampled; at
those positions siblings diverge only about half the time. Where divergence is
certain, the local term is identically zero and the visitation prefactor is maximal.


The statement we can therefore make is stronger than ``the local term is
incomplete'': \emph{the local term is weakest exactly where the term it omits is strongest}, and
the omission cannot be fixed within the local channel.

\subsection{Estimating the Visitation Channel}
\label{sec:ahdef-app}

\subsubsection{Causal elimination gives a reward-to-go}

The visitation term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:twochannel} is not yet in a usable form: the
factor $f_\theta(y)$ multiplying the score is a property of the \emph{whole}
trajectory, so a naive estimator would credit every token with entropy that appeared
before it. Causality removes those terms exactly.

Expanding both factors --- $f_\theta$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:fdef} and the score by
$\gradt \log \pit(y \mid x) = \sum_u \gradt \log \pit(y_u \mid s_u)$ --- turns (V)
into a double sum:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(\mathrm{V}) = \sum_t \sum_u
\E\bigl[&H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))\\
&\cdot \gradt \log \pit(y_u \mid s_u)\bigr].
\end{split}
\label{eq:doublesum}
\end{equation}
%
Consider a term with $u \ge t$. The entropy $H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$ depends on $y$
only through $y_{<t}$, and $y_{<t} \subseteq y_{<u}$, so it is measurable with
respect to $y_{<u}$ and may be pulled out of a conditional expectation given
$y_{<u}$. What remains is the expected score at a single position, which vanishes
for any distribution:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\E\bigl[\gradt &\log \pit(y_u \mid s_u) \mid y_{<u}\bigr]\\
&= \sum_a \pit(a \mid s_u) \frac{\gradt \pit(a \mid s_u)}{\pit(a \mid s_u)}\\
&= \gradt \!\sum_a \pit(a \mid s_u)
\;=\; \gradt 1 \;=\; 0 .
\end{split}
\label{eq:scorezero}
\end{equation}
%
By the tower rule every $(t, u)$ term with $u \ge t$ is therefore \emph{exactly}
zero --- not small, zero. This is the same identity that licenses a baseline in
policy gradients, used here to enforce causality rather than to reduce variance.

Only $u < t$ survives. Swapping the order of summation collects, for each token
$u$, the entropies at all later positions:
%
\begin{equation}
(\mathrm{V}) = \E\Bigl[\sum_u \gradt \log \pit(y_u \mid s_u)\,
\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u)\Bigr],
\label{eq:togo-app}
\end{equation}
%
with $\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) := \sum_{t > u} H(\pit(\cdot \mid s_t))$.

We stress that $\Htogo$ was not posited. Equation~\eqref{eq:togo} is a REINFORCE
estimator whose reward is ``the conditional entropy remaining after this token'',
and $\Htogo$ is that reward-to-go.

\subsubsection{The sibling-prefix baseline}

Any function $b(s_u)$ of the state alone leaves Eq.~\eqref{eq:togo} unbiased, by the
same argument. Subtracting one gives the per-token advantage
%
\begin{equation}
\AH(s_u, y_u) := \Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) - \bar{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}(s_u),
\label{eq:ah-app}
\end{equation}
%
and up to this point \emph{no approximation has been made}.

The baseline we use is the mean of $\Htogo$ over the rollouts in the same prompt
group whose prefixes $y_{<u}$ agree exactly. Those rollouts share the state $s_u$ by
definition, so their mean is a function of $s_u$ alone and the baseline is
admissible. The condition is agreement on $y_{<u}$ and not on $y_{\le u}$: the
rollouts must diverge \emph{at} $u$ for the difference to be a branch score.

Two properties follow immediately and matter later. First, the implementation
includes a rollout in its own sibling set, so $b$ depends weakly on $y_u$ and the
estimator is not strictly leave-one-out; the resulting bias is $O(1/m)$ in the
number $m$ of surviving siblings, the same property GRPO's group-mean advantage
has. Second, when $m = 1$ the baseline equals the rollout's own value and
$\AH \equiv 0$, so a position with no siblings is silent rather than noisy.

\subsection{Four Approximations}
\label{sec:approx}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ah} is exact but not computable: $\Htogo$ sums conditional
entropies at states the update has not visited. Three approximations make it
computable and a fourth bounds its influence.

\subsubsection{A1: horizon truncation and discounting}

We truncate the sum to $\kappa$ steps and discount:
%
\begin{equation}
\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u) \approx
\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa} \gamma_H^{\,k}\, H\bigl(\pi(\cdot \mid s_{u+k})\bigr).
\label{eq:a1}
\end{equation}
%
The discount serves two purposes: distant terms carry more forecast error (A2) and
therefore deserve less trust, and the sum stays bounded independently of $\kappa$.

What this discards --- the tail $\sum_{k > \kappa}$ and a fraction of the retained
terms --- is largely harmless for a reason specific to our use. Equation~\eqref{eq:ah}
consumes only the \emph{difference} between siblings. Any bias common to rollouts
sharing $s_u$ cancels in that difference, and truncation bias is common to first
order.

\subsubsection{A2: forecasting the unrealized future}

Even Eq.~\eqref{eq:a1} requires the future states $s_{u+k}$. At update time they do
not exist: rollouts came from $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$, each state has exactly one
sampled continuation, and re-rolling is prohibitive. We replace the realized future
with a parallel forecast from the current hidden state $h_u$:
%
\begin{equation}
H\bigl(\pi(\cdot \mid s_{u+k})\bigr) \;\longrightarrow\;
H\bigl(p^{(k)}_{\mathrm{MTP}}(\cdot \mid h_u)\bigr).
\label{eq:a2}
\end{equation}
%
Head $k$ is a two-layer residual MLP applied to $h_u$, followed by the policy's own
unembedding; the heads own no vocabulary parameters. Their last layer is
zero-initialized, so before any training every head reproduces the policy's own
next-token distribution and the forecast starts from a sane value. In the RL path the
logits are materialized in chunks and reduced to an entropy immediately, so the
$[\kappa, B, T, V]$ tensor never exists.

\paragraph{The error of this substitution has a known sign.}
Conditioning on the intervening tokens is exactly what the forecast omits. Fixing
$S_u$, the $k$-th term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:a1} has expectation
$H(Y_{u+k} \mid Y_{u+1:u+k-1}, S_u)$, whereas the MTP head models the
\emph{marginal} $p(Y_{u+k} \mid S_u)$. Since conditioning reduces entropy,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
H(Y_{u+k} \mid S_u) &- \E\bigl[H(\pi(\cdot \mid S_{u+k-1}))\bigr]\\
&= I\bigl(Y_{u+k};\, Y_{u+1:u+k-1} \mid S_u\bigr)\\
&\ge 0 .
\end{split}
\label{eq:mi}
\end{equation}
%
The forecast \emph{always over-estimates}, and the excess is precisely the mutual
information with the intervening tokens, which grows monotonically with $k$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:mi} is not merely a bound: it is the reason the marginal is the
quantity we want. It is the fan-out of the continuation --- how many ways the next
$k$ tokens can unfold --- rather than the residual uncertainty along the one path
that happened to be sampled.

\subsubsection{A3: per-head affine calibration}

Equation~\eqref{eq:mi} predicts a per-head, monotone inflation, so we absorb it with
a per-head affine correction fitted by least squares against oracle entropies on
warm-up rollouts:
%
\begin{equation}
\hat{H}_k = a_k\, H\bigl(p^{(k)}_{\mathrm{MTP}}(\cdot \mid h_u)\bigr) + b_k .
\label{eq:a3}
\end{equation}
%
The fitted coefficients confirm the prediction. On our model the first head has
$a_1 = 1.03$ --- a one-step forecast has no intervening tokens, so the mutual
information in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mi} is zero and the approximation is exact --- while
$a_k$ drops to $0.10$--$0.19$ for $k \ge 2$. Calibration switches the unreliable
heads off on its own.

Combining A1--A3 gives the forecast that is actually computed:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}(s_u \oplus y_u) =\\
&\qquad \max\Bigl(0,\ \sum_{k=1}^{\kappa} \gamma_H^{\,k}\bigl(a_k H_k(h_u) + b_k\bigr)\Bigr),
\end{split}
\label{eq:forecast-app}
\end{equation}
%
the clamp encoding the fact that an entropy cannot be negative. With
$\kappa = 2$ and $\gamma_H = 0.7$ this instantiates to
$\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}} = 0.72\,H_1 + 0.06\,H_2 + 0.11$: the second head
contributes about $8\%$, so the effective horizon is close to one step. We regard
this as a consequence of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mi} rather than a tuning artifact, and note
it as a limitation --- a forecaster whose error did not grow with $k$ would leave a
longer horizon usable.

\paragraph{Alignment.}
One detail decides whether the signal is a branch score at all. The hidden state at
position $u$ is conditioned on $s_u$ and predicts $y_u$; it does not know $y_u$. The
quantity we need, $\Htogo(s_u \oplus y_u)$, must therefore be read from position
$u+1$ and shifted back by one. Omitting the shift measures the value of the state
\emph{before} the branch rather than the value of the branch taken.

\subsection{From Gradient to Token Weights}
\label{sec:weights}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ah} is a term to add to a gradient, but methods in this family do
not add gradients --- they re-weight a loss. What is needed is a first-order
prediction of how much one update, acting through token $u$, changes the trajectory
entropy.

\paragraph{How far one step moves a logit.}
We reuse the logit-space update of \citet[Eq.~23]{steer} rather than rederiving it.
With $w_u = \mathrm{clip}(r_u, 1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}, 1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{hi}})A_u$
the coefficient the policy loss actually applies, Assumption~\ref{ass:indep} makes a
single step move only the sampled token's logit,
$\Delta z_{u,a} = \eta\, w_u\, \delta_{a, y_u} + O(\eta^2)$.
The softmax Jacobian $\partial \log \pi_a / \partial z_a = 1 - \pi_a$ then gives
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u = \eta\, w_u\,(1 - \pi_u),
\label{eq:dlogpi}
\end{equation}
%
which vanishes as $\pi_u \to 1$: a token that already holds the mass cannot be given
more. This is the prefactor whose anti-correlation with $|\Om|$ Table~\ref{tab:anticorr}
records.

\paragraph{The visitation-channel prediction.}
Because $\gradt \log \pi_u \cdot \Delta\theta = \Delta \log \pi_u$, substituting into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ah} gives the per-token quantity
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{visit}_u := \widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u \cdot
\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\AH(s_u,y_u), -c, +c\bigr).
\end{split}
\label{eq:visit}
\end{equation}
%
\textbf{A4} is the clip, and it is applied to $\AH$ \emph{before} the product rather
than after, so that one token's influence is bounded by $|\eta w_u| \cdot c$.
Clipping the product instead would let forecast error be amplified without bound at
tokens with large $w_u$.

\paragraph{Sign quadrants.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:visit} defines the collapse channel precisely.
When $\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u > 0$ and $\AH > 0$, mass moves toward a branch
richer than its siblings and trajectory entropy rises. When
$\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u > 0$ and $\AH < 0$, mass moves into a dead end and the
diversity carried by the abandoned siblings is destroyed --- this is the collapse
path that (L) is structurally unable to see. Negative
$\widehat{\Delta \log \pi}_u$ reverses both readings.

\subsection{Combining the Channels}
\label{sec:combine}

The visitation term could be added to $\Om$ before the weight map, but measured on
real rollouts that map destroys the signal: $\Om$ carries a factor
$1/\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$, so $\max|\Om| / \mathrm{median}|\Om|$ reaches $3\times10^{4}$
and the band is spent on one or two outliers while the bulk lands on a point. We
therefore apply the correction \emph{after} the map, as a bounded adjustment of the
weights the base method has already produced.

Let $\mathcal{S} = \{u : \mathrm{visit}_u \neq 0\}$ be the support --- by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branchonly}, the branch points --- and let
$b = \lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min}$ be the width of the weight band. For
$u \in \mathcal{S}$,
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\alpha^{\mathrm{f}}_u = \mathrm{clamp}\Bigl(&\alpha_u
  + \lambda\, b\, \tanh\!\bigl(\mathrm{visit}_u / \rho\bigr),\\
  &\lambda_{\min},\ \lambda_{\max}\Bigr),
\end{split}
\label{eq:combine-app}
\end{equation}
%
with $\rho = \mathrm{rms}\{\mathrm{visit}_v : v \in \mathcal{S}\}$;
$\alpha^{\mathrm{f}}_u = \alpha_u$ elsewhere.

Three properties matter. The $\tanh$ is odd, monotone and bounded, so it preserves
the sign of the visitation term, caps one token's movement at $\lambda b$, and does
not depend on the absolute scale of $\mathrm{visit}$. The RMS is taken over
$\mathcal{S}$ alone rather than over the whole response: normalizing by the whole
response would shrink the correction in proportion to how sparse branch points are,
which is a property of the sampler and not of the signal. And at $\lambda = 0$ the
correction vanishes identically, so the pipeline is bitwise identical to the base
method --- a regression test enforces this.

Because the map runs first and the correction second, the step size $\eta$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dlogpi} enters only through $\mathrm{visit}_u / \rho$, where it
cancels. We fix $\eta = 1$ and sweep $\lambda$ alone.

\paragraph{The support is part of the treatment.}
$\mathcal{S}$ is defined by $\mathrm{visit}_u \neq 0$ rather than by a threshold,
deliberately: it selects both where the correction lands and, through $\rho$, how
large every correction is, so a tighter test would change what is trained rather than
what is logged. One consequence is that $\mathcal{S}$ admits float32 round-off ---
$\AH$ is a deviation from a mean over token-identical siblings, and
$\sum_j g_j / m$ does not reproduce $g_i$ bitwise, leaving $|\AH| \sim 6\times10^{-8}$
where it is interpretively zero. Those residuals are $\sim\!10^{-6}$ relative to a
real branch score and cannot move a weight, but they are counted in the support
statistics of Section~\ref{sec:res:diag}, which should be read as an upper bound on
the branch-point fraction.

\subsection{Tree-Structured Rollouts}
\label{sec:tree}

\subsubsection{Why plain rollouts cannot support $\AH$}

$\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}$ is a deterministic function of the causal prefix, so two
rollouts sharing $y_{\le u}$ produce identical forecasts. Combined with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ah} this gives a sharp characterization:
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\AH[i, u] \neq 0 \iff\ &\text{siblings share } y_{<u}\\
&\text{but diverge at } y_u,
\end{split}
\label{eq:branchonly-app}
\end{equation}
%
that is, $u$ is a branch point of the group. Under independent sampling from a
sharpened policy, group members diverge at their first token and never share a
prefix again, so $m = 1$ almost everywhere and $\AH \equiv 0$: the visitation
channel is not merely weak, it is identically absent.

We therefore schedule the sharing. A tree sampler expands a single root and cuts it
at fixed depths $d_1 < d_2 < d_3$ with branching factor $2$, yielding $n = 8$ leaves
whose sibling structure is known in advance. The cuts create the only positions at
which Eq.~\eqref{eq:branchonly} can fire. This is the smallest change to the sampler
that lets the estimator exist, and it is all we ask of the tree: the depths are
fixed rather than chosen, and we do not tune or claim the schedule as a way to
sample.

\subsubsection{A ceiling on the support}

It is tempting to treat the fraction of positions with $\AH \neq 0$ as a quantity to
be maximized. It is not, and the reason is combinatorial rather than empirical. Two
quantities behave very differently under the tree:

\begin{itemize}
\item the fraction of positions where \emph{some} other rollout shares $y_{<u}$ is
      moved by the cuts, and equals $d_L/T$ by construction;
\item the fraction where $\AH \neq 0$ is \emph{not}, because by
      Eq.~\eqref{eq:branchonly} it counts branch points, and $n$ sequences form a
      trie with at most $n-1$ internal branching nodes.
\end{itemize}

Since the one-position alignment shift widens each branch node into two columns, the
fraction of positions carrying a non-zero $\AH$ is bounded by $2(n-1)/T$ for
\emph{any} sampler whatsoever. With $n = 8$ and responses of $\sim 10^3$ tokens this
ceiling is on the order of a percent. The tree's role is to make the branch points
exist and to place them at controlled depths, not to make them numerous.

\subsubsection{The correction cannot move the mean}
\label{sec:zerosum-app}

$\AH$ is a deviation from a sibling mean, so on each sibling set
%
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j \in \mathrm{sib}(s_u)} \AH\bigl(s_u, y^{(j)}_u\bigr) = 0 .
\label{eq:zerosum}
\end{equation}
%
The correction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:combine} therefore cannot change the average weight
assigned to branch tokens. It can only spread siblings apart. This is not a defect
to be engineered away: an unbiased estimator requires a baseline, and a baseline
forces the mean to zero.

The consequence for evaluation is direct, and we state it before presenting any
experiment. ``Mean entropy at branch points increased'' is \emph{not} a prediction of
this method and cannot serve as evidence that it works; the corresponding prediction
is that the \emph{variance across siblings} widens. A method designed to raise the
mean is a different and weaker hypothesis --- it does not need a forecast at all,
only a support --- and we include it below as a control arm.

The strongest assumptions are A2 and B2. A2 has a known sign and a known monotonicity

\section{Experimental Details}
\label{sec:setup-app}
\subsection{Model, data, and optimization}

All runs fine-tune the same base model, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B \citep{yang2024aqwen}, on the same data with
the same optimizer settings, and differ only in the intervention; the five-arm
decomposition and every ablation are run on it. We run no second backbone, and the
Limitations say what that leaves open. All runs train on the same $17$k-problem
competition-mathematics set, DAPO-Math-17k \citep{yu2025dapo}. With a training batch of $512$ prompts,
$110$ optimization steps correspond to about $3.2$ epochs. Two further runs of the
full method at $200$ steps, under the base method's own protocol, are described
separately in Section~\ref{sec:reported}.


\subsection{Validation set}
\label{sec:valset}

Every run validates on AIME 2024 ($30$ problems, $32$ samples each) every $10$ steps.
AIME 2024 is small: with a per-problem accuracy spread of $\num{STDaime}$ measured
across our runs, the standard error of its mean is
$\num{STDaime}/\sqrt{30} \approx \num{SEaime}$. That figure governs two things.
It bounds what any single validation point can resolve, which is why the primary
statistic below is a window mean rather than one point; and it bounds checkpoint
selection, since a benchmark this noisy chooses between nearby checkpoints partly by
chance. Where a checkpoint is selected (the six-benchmark evaluation of
Section~\ref{sec:res:bench}), the AIME24 column is therefore marked in-sample.

Optimization uses GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} with $n = 8$ responses per prompt, a constant learning rate
of $1\mathrm{e}{-6}$ with no warm-up (so that the schedule is independent of the
declared total step count), a maximum response length of $3072$ tokens, no entropy
bonus, and no KL loss. The clipped surrogate \citep{schulman2017proximal} is
asymmetric, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.2$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.28$ as in \citet{yu2025dapo}, with
the dual-clip lower bound at $c = 10$; we state this because ``GRPO'' with a
symmetric $\varepsilon = 0.2$ and a KL term is a different baseline, and because
the follow-up arm of Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate} is defined by moving these two
numbers. Every arm, the baseline included, uses the same three values: the
only configuration difference between the GRPO arm and STEER-F is the loss mode and
the method hyperparameters listed next. Rollouts are sampled at temperature $1.0$ with no nucleus
truncation; validation uses temperature $1.0$ and $\mathrm{top\text{-}}p = 0.7$.
Every arm uses the same data seed and the same initial checkpoint, which we verify by
requiring the step-$0$ validation scores to agree.

\subsection{Method hyperparameters}

The forecaster uses $\kappa = 2$ heads with discount $\gamma_H = 0.7$ and the
per-head calibration of Eq.~\eqref{eq:a3}. The combination uses $\lambda = 0.25$,
RMS normalization, clip $c = 1.0$, and the sibling-prefix baseline. The weight map
is the base method's exponential map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:steermap}, taken
un-rescaled, at $\lambda_{\min} = 0.7$, so weights land in $[0.7, 1]$ and can only
attenuate. This is the value the base method's paper sets, inside the range $[0.6, 0.8]$ over
which it reports the method performing consistently
\citep[App.~F.1]{steer}; it was taken from there and not tuned on these runs. The
base method's released launcher uses $\lambda_{\min} = 0.8$, and every other value
in that launcher matches ours.\footnote{Our implementation exposes an optional reshaping of
$\Omt$ before this map (winsorizing or rank-transforming it); every run reported
here uses the identity setting, so the base method's map sees exactly what it would
have seen.} The tree sampler uses a single root, cuts at depths
$\{64, 192, 384\}$, and branching factor $2$ at each cut, giving $n = 8$ leaves.

\subsection{Five arms}

The design isolates one question: does the \emph{value} of the forecast matter, or
only the apparatus that carries it? A comparison between the base method and the
full method changes two things at once ($\lambda$ and the sampler), so it cannot
answer this. We therefore add two arms that keep the entire apparatus --- the tree,
the forecaster, the support, the weight mapping --- and destroy only the information
in $\AH$.


\textsc{uniform} applies the same attenuation to every position in the support,
discarding $\AH$'s value but keeping its support --- this is the implementation of
the ``raise the mean'' hypothesis that Section~\ref{sec:zerosum} distinguishes from
ours. \textsc{permuted} keeps the multiset of $\AH$ values within each sibling set and
shuffles the assignment, so the support, the magnitudes, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:zerosum}
all survive while the pairing between a branch and its score is destroyed. Together
they bracket the claim: if the visitation signal is doing the work, both controls
should behave like the no-forecast arms.

\subsection{Metrics}

We report $\mathrm{acc@}32$ (mean accuracy over $32$ samples) and $\mathrm{maj@}32$
(accuracy of the majority vote over the same $32$ samples), and their difference
%
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{uplift} := \mathrm{maj@}32 - \mathrm{acc@}32,
\end{equation*}
%
which measures how much better a set of $32$ samples is than its average member ---
that is, whether correct answers concentrate while incorrect ones scatter. We use
uplift rather than $\mathrm{pass@}32$ because the latter does not separate the arms.

Because single validation points are noisy on a $30$-problem set, the primary
statistic is the mean over the converged window, steps $40$--$110$ ($8$ validation
points per arm). We quote differences in units of the across-problem standard error,
$\mathrm{SE} = \mathrm{std@}32 / \sqrt{30} \approx 0.027$, which is the correct scale
for generalization to new problems. We separately note that the pure re-sampling
noise of the protocol --- estimated from two validations of identical weights --- is
about $0.004$ in accuracy, an order of magnitude smaller; the two scales answer
different questions and we are explicit about which is in use.

One instrumentation caveat propagates into every entropy number we report. The
logged \texttt{actor/entropy} is aggregated with a token-mean, so it is
$\Htraj$ divided by the mean response length, not $\Htraj$. Since response lengths
differ by under $5\%$ across arms, we report the raw quantity and, where the
distinction could matter, the length-corrected product as well.

\subsection{Entropy: the aggregate moves with the apparatus, not with $\AH$}
\label{sec:res:entropy-app}


\paragraph{What the method predicts here, fixed in advance.} This method does not
set out to hold the aggregate entropy up, and two facts already established above
say it cannot. Section~\ref{sec:tree} bounds the positions at which $\AH$ can be
non-zero at $2(n-1)/T$, about one in eighty; Section~\ref{sec:zerosum} shows $\AH$
sums to zero over a sibling set, so at the positions it does reach it redistributes
weight among siblings rather than adding any. An intervention that is this sparse
and mean-preserving by construction should leave an average over every response
token close to where it found it. Both statements were written into the manuscript
before the campaign was launched, Section~\ref{sec:zerosum} explicitly so
(``we state it before presenting any experiment''), and the artifact history dates
them an hour ahead of the pre-registration discussed below.

\paragraph{What the seeds show.} Table~\ref{tab:entropy} groups the contrasts by
what each one changes, and the grouping separates them cleanly. The two contrasts
that change the apparatus move the aggregate: turning the damping on costs
$\num{Csteergrpoentropy}$ at $t = \num{Tsteergrpoentropy}$, and adding the tree with
damping applied uniformly returns $\num{Cuniformsteerentropy}$ at
$t = \num{Tuniformsteerentropy}$ --- the latter reproducing across all
$\num{Nseedsused}$ seeds to within a thousandth. The contrasts that hold the
apparatus fixed and change only what $\AH$ contains move it by
$\num{Csigneduniformentropy}$ ($t = \num{Tsigneduniformentropy}$) when its value is
discarded and $\num{Csignedpermutedentropy}$ when its pairing is destroyed. Accuracy
runs the other way: the apparatus contrasts give $\num{Cuniformsteeracc}$ and
$\num{Csteergrpoacc}$, while the two $\AH$ contrasts give $\num{Csigneduniformacc}$
and $\num{Csignedpermutedacc}$. The quantity that moves the aggregate entropy is the
damping apparatus; the quantity that moves accuracy is $\AH$. That is the predicted
pattern, and it is the sense in which this paper is not a method for suppressing
entropy.

\paragraph{A pre-registered condition we retire rather than score.} The
pre-registration (Section~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}) also fixed a condition on this
section: if aggregate entropy orders the arms the same way accuracy does, a
dissociation argument fails. We retire that condition instead of reporting it,
and say plainly why, since retiring a registered test is exactly the move a reader
should be suspicious of. First, it tests the aggregate as a signal about $\AH$,
which the two paragraphs above --- and Section~\ref{sec:zerosum}, committed an hour
before the registration was --- say it cannot be. Second, an ordering over five arm
means is not stable at this seed count: the rank correlation between converged
entropy and converged accuracy comes out with a different sign depending on whether
each arm is averaged over the seed common to all five (Table~\ref{tab:plateau}) or
over its own, which differ in number while the campaign runs. Both seed sets are in
the released \texttt{per\_seed.tsv}. Third, and because of the first two, \emph{we
claim nothing from it in either direction}: the condition is not reported as passed,
and the theory is not reported as damaged by it. It was the wrong instrument to have
registered, and the honest correction is to say so and to register a better one.

\paragraph{The measurement that is actually owed.} Section~\ref{sec:zerosum} states
the prediction that a sum-zero correction does make: not that entropy at branch
points rises, but that the \emph{spread across siblings} widens. No quantity we
currently log tests it. The trainer does log an entropy split
(\texttt{steerf/branch\_entropy}), and an earlier draft of this section read it as
entropy at branch points; it is not. That monitor takes a fixed top decile of
$\AH$ --- a tenth of all response tokens --- while the positions $\AH$ can reach are
the one-in-eighty that Section~\ref{sec:res:diag} measures, an eighth as many. Since
$\AH$ is exactly zero at almost every position, the remainder of that decile is
filled by an arbitrary tie-break rather than by branching. Section~\ref{sec:res:diag} reports
what that monitor does and does not select. The replacement restricts the split to
$\AH \ne 0$, the same predicate the correction itself uses, and is instrumented in
the release; no run reported here carries it, so this test is registered and not
performed. That test is registered
in Section~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}, before those runs, and we state here that it
is registered after this section's original condition was retired rather than
before.

\paragraph{What the numbers above cannot carry.} The claim is about magnitudes, not
about one row failing a threshold. Changing the apparatus moves the aggregate by
$\num{Cuniformsteerentropy}$ and $\num{Csteergrpoentropy}$; changing what $\AH$
contains moves it by $\num{Csignedpermutedentropy}$ and
$\num{Csigneduniformentropy}$, and the smaller of the two apparatus effects is still
more than twice the larger of the two $\AH$ effects. The $|t|$ bands separate the
same way and do not overlap --- $\num{Tsteergrpoentropy}$ and
$\num{Tuniformsteerentropy}$ against $\num{Tsigneduniformentropy}$ and
$\num{Tsignedpermutedentropy}$ --- which is why the argument is the contrast
\emph{between groups} rather than any single row. Two cautions travel with it. A
small difference that reproduces across seeds can still reach a large $t$: the
$\AH$ contrast on \textsc{permuted} does, at $\num{Csignedpermutedentropy}$, and
that is a statement about consistency, not about size. And $\num{Nsigneduniform}$
seeds entitle us to say the aggregate does not move \emph{much} when $\AH$ changes,
not that it is unchanged.

\subsection{Where the gain comes from}
\label{sec:res:decomp}

The majority-vote gain decomposes into a part explained by per-sample accuracy and a
part explained by the sample set's structure:
%
\begin{equation*}
\underbrace{\num{Csignedgrpomaj}}_{\Delta\mathrm{maj}}
=\underbrace{\num{Csignedgrpoacc}}_{\Delta\mathrm{acc}}
+\underbrace{\num{Csignedgrpouplift}}_{\Delta\mathrm{uplift}},
\end{equation*}
%
against GRPO; against \textsc{steer} the uplift term is
$\num{Csignedsteeruplift}$ ($t = \num{Tsignedsteeruplift}$ over
$\num{Nsignedsteer}$ seeds). Against GRPO roughly two thirds of the improvement is
not that individual samples are more often right, but that the right ones agree with
each other more and the wrong ones agree less. Both terms are
means over the same $\num{Nseedsused}$ seeds, and the split inherits the weakness of
the gap it splits: against \textsc{steer} the uplift term is smaller and well
inside its own spread, and against GRPO the whole gap is inside GRPO's.

Where that structural change comes from is a separate question, and the arm that
answers it finished after this section was first written. \textsc{uniform} runs the
sampler at $\lambda = .25$ with the forecast's value destroyed and shows no uplift
gain, which reads as though the structure belonged to $\AH$. It does not: the
$\lambda = 0$ tree arm --- the sampler with the future channel off, \textsc{steer}'s
local damping still in place --- reaches an uplift
of $\num{Rlamzerouplift}$, against $\num{Rsteeruplift}$ for \textsc{steer} and
$\num{Rsigneduplift}$ for STEER-F. On this evidence most of the structural gain is
the sampler's, and Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate} reports it that way. The
decomposition above is unaffected --- it is measured against GRPO, which has no tree
--- but the attribution is not what the five-arm design alone could see. A gain that
belongs to the sampler is outside what this paper set out to study, since the tree is
here only to give $\AH$ its siblings; we report it and leave its explanation to work
that studies structured sampling directly.

\subsection{Mechanism diagnostics}
\label{sec:res:diag}

Section~\ref{sec:tree} bounds the fraction of positions that can carry a non-zero
$\AH$ at $2(n-1)/T$ for any sampler. With $n = 8$ and the tree arms' mean response
length of $\num{Treelen}$ tokens over the converged window the bound is
$\num{Supportbound}$. Measured over the same window and seeds, the three tree arms
sit at $\num{Runiformbranchfrac}$ (\textsc{uniform}), $\num{Rpermutedbranchfrac}$
(\textsc{permuted}) and $\num{Rsignedbranchfrac}$ (STEER-F), i.e.\ at
$\num{Supportpctlo}$--$\num{Supportpcthi}\%$ of a ceiling that no sampler can
exceed. A plain-rollout pilot with the same $\lambda$ (seed $1$, released with the
logs) reaches $.003$. As Section~\ref{sec:combine} notes, this
count admits float32 round-off and is an upper bound; the runs reported here predate
the round-off-free counter, so we quote the loose figure and label it as such.

Two readings follow. The tree sampler is close to saturating what the combinatorics
allow, so there is little headroom in scheduling more cuts; and the intervention is
extremely sparse --- about one position in eighty --- which makes the size of the
effect in Table~\ref{tab:contrast} the surprising part rather than the small support.

That sparsity also disqualifies one of our own monitors, which we record here rather
than leave for a reader to find. The logged entropy split
(\texttt{steerf/branch\_entropy} and its gap) selects the top decile of $\AH$ by
rank, a tenth of all response tokens, against a support an eighth that size. A
rank-based cut was chosen deliberately --- $\AH$ is exactly zero wherever a rollout
has become its own only sibling, so a value-based threshold selects almost
everything --- but with a support this small the decile is filled out by the
tie-break among those zeros, and the tie-break is not a random sample of them.
A top-decile cut does recall about half of the true divergence points, five times
what selecting at random would give, so the bucket is genuinely enriched; it is
nonetheless mostly not branch positions, and the mean entropy it reports cannot be
attributed to them. Section~\ref{sec:res:entropy} states what replaces it.

\subsection{Is the future term a local term?}
\label{sec:res:locality}

Section~\ref{sec:approx} reports a calibration that suppresses every head past
the first, leaving $\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}$ dominated by one step ahead. That
invites the sharpest objection to the paper: if the forecast reaches one step,
is $\AH$ anything a purely local quantity could not have produced? The question
is answerable without training anything. We apply the \emph{same} operator ---
the same sibling baseline, mask and grouping --- to two inputs: the calibrated
forecast, and the policy's own next-token entropy read at the same offset, the
state after the branch token. Any difference between the two is the
forecaster's contribution and nothing else. We report it on the sibling
support, where $\AH$ can be non-zero at all; outside it both are identically
zero and a statistic over all positions measures the support rather than the
forecast.

The answer is that the forecaster is not earning its cost. Over
\num{Locn} branch points from \num{Locgroups} validation prompts, the two
advantages correlate at $r = \num{Locadv}$ (Spearman $\num{Locadvrho}$), the
local twin explains $R^2 = \num{Locrsq}$ of the treatment, and the two agree in
sign $\num{Locsign}$ of the time --- and the sign is what the token weights are
monotone in. The levels correlate less than the advantages
($\num{Loclevels}$), which is the wrong way round for an artefact of scale.

The mechanism is one the paper had already measured without connecting it. With
$\kappa = 2$ and $\gamma_H = 0.7$ the calibration instantiates to
$\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}} = 0.72\,H_1 + 0.06\,H_2 + 0.11$, the sibling baseline
removes the constant, and the first head is built to reproduce the policy's own
next-token distribution --- its final layer is zero-initialised and its fitted
$a_1 = 1.03$ says it stayed there. So $\AH$ should be close to
$\gamma_H a_1 = 0.72$ times the local twin, and the measured magnitudes are
$\num{Locahmag}$ against $\num{Locloctwinmag}$, a ratio of $\num{Locratio}$.
$\AH$ is very nearly a scalar multiple of a quantity the trainer already
computes, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:combine} divides the scale out through
$\rho = \operatorname{rms}\{\mathrm{visit}\}$.

Two things this does \emph{not} say. It does not touch
Eq.~\eqref{eq:twochannel}, which is an identity, nor
Section~\ref{sec:blind}: the local twin is not $\Om$. $\Om$ carries no
sibling baseline and no tree, vanishes at a uniform branch point, and the twin
exists only because the tree supplies siblings to difference against. What the
measurement indicts is the \emph{forecasting apparatus} --- the MTP heads,
their warm-up and their calibration --- not the visitation channel. Read
alongside Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate}, where the sampler alone recovers
$\num{Rlamzeroacc}$ against \textsc{steer}'s $\num{Rsteeracc}$ while the
forecast adds the remainder, two independent measurements now point the same
way.

The caveats are the ones a single diagnostic run carries: \num{Locgroups}
prompts at one validation step of the base policy, on independently sampled
rollouts, whose sibling support therefore sits early in the response rather
than at the scheduled branch depths. The head-free arm of
Table~\ref{tab:followups} is the direct test, and it has now been run.
\textsc{oracle} reads $\Htogo$ from the policy's realised entropy, which verl
\citep{sheng2025hybridflow} computes in the same pass, so it carries none of the MTP overhead of
Section~\ref{sec:res:cost} --- $\num{Roraclecost}$\,s per optimization step
against STEER-F's $\num{Fsignedoracleref}$ at the same seed on the four-GPU box,
$\num{Fsignedoraclesaving}\%$ less. It is also
behind it on accuracy. Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate} reports both numbers and what
one seed of each can carry.

\subsection{Seeds, not steps}
\label{sec:res:seeds}

Every statistic above treats the seed as the unit of analysis: each run is first
collapsed to one number by averaging its converged window, the arms are then paired
within a seed, and the test is a one-sample $t$ on the paired differences with
$\mathrm{df} = n - 1$. Pairing within a seed matters because a seed carries effects
common to all five arms --- the data order, and on a split campaign the machine a run
landed on --- and those cancel exactly in a difference. A contrast's $n$ is the
smaller of its two arms' seed counts, which is why the table reports $n$ per row
rather than once.

The alternative statistic, a paired $t$ over the eight window steps \emph{within} one
run, measures whether a gap is stable along a single trajectory. That is a different
question from whether it survives re-running, and we report it only in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-within} so the two are not confused.

\paragraph{Mechanical failures, and what replaced them.} The pre-registration
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}) excludes a run for mechanical failure and
relaunches it with the same seed. STEER-F at seed $4$ stopped at training step $91$
when its machine lost a GPU; it was relaunched at seed $4$ and the relaunch, which
reached step $110$, is the run reported. STEER-F at seed $2$ failed at start-up on a
missing module and was not relaunched within this campaign's budget, which is why
seed $2$ is outside the seed set. Both failed runs are in the release, flagged
rather than deleted.

\paragraph{A seed index also names a machine.} A seed of this campaign ran on a
two-GPU box or a four-GPU one, uniformly across arms, and the seed set this paper
uses --- $\num{Seedset}$ --- holds $\num{Boxnlo}$ of the first and $\num{Boxnhi}$ of
the second for every arm. The effect is measurable on $\num{Boxarm}$, which has both:
$\num{Boxlo}$ on the smaller box against $\num{Boxhi}$ on the larger, a difference of
$\num{Boxdelta}$, larger than the gap between the top two rows of
Table~\ref{tab:plateau}.

Read that difference as an upper bound and not as an estimate. With
$\num{Boxnlo}$ run on the smaller box it is one run against $\num{Boxnhi}$, and a
single run carries this arm's own run-to-run spread (Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds}
below) inside it. We report it at that strength because the alternative was to keep a
seed outside $\num{Seedset}$ alive for this one number while every other number in
the paper excluded it, and a nuisance estimate is not worth a second seed set.

What the figure does not reach is the comparisons, for two reasons that do not depend
on how well it is estimated. The contrasts pair inside a seed and therefore inside a
machine, so whatever a box is worth cancels in the pair. And every arm in
Table~\ref{tab:plateau} carries the same $\num{Boxnlo}$-to-$\num{Boxnhi}$ mix, so a
row difference does not carry a box mix either. The release prints the per-arm machine
mix on every run of the analysis.

\paragraph{Run-to-run spread, and it is the number that governs this section.}
Every arm has now finished more than once. GRPO's converged-window accuracy moves
from \num{Logrpoacc} to \num{Higrpoacc} across its \num{Nallgrpo} seeds, a spread
of \num{Sgrpoacc}; STEER-F moves from \num{Losignedacc} to \num{Hisignedacc} across
its \num{Nallsigned} seeds, a spread of \num{Ssignedacc}; \textsc{permuted}
moves only \num{Spermutedacc} across \num{Nallpermuted}. Both extremes are
measured on the same benchmark with the same protocol, and they differ by more than
an order of magnitude.

Set the headline gap of \num{Csignedgrpoacc} against those. \textbf{It is a fraction
of GRPO's own spread across seeds and several times STEER-F's}, so the two arms it
compares do not even agree on how large a seed-to-seed difference is, and each is
observed a handful of times. That is the regime in which a single paired difference
cannot decide the question, and we do not claim it does. A range between three draws
is also not a standard error: the largest of these ranges is more than ten times the
smallest, so they do not bound each other and no contrast here should be read against
a noise floor quoted to one significant figure. For scale at the other end,
two validations of the \emph{same} weights on these $30$ questions differ by about
$.004$, so seed variation is the larger term by roughly an order of magnitude and
any comparison quoted at single-run precision is quoting noise.

We therefore state what this experiment establishes and what it does not. The
mechanism contrasts --- what changes when $\AH$'s value is discarded or its pairing
destroyed, with the apparatus held fixed --- are the claim. The effect size would need more seeds than
this campaign has, and on the seeds it has the headline gap is not separated from
the spread.

\subsection{Six benchmarks}
\label{sec:res:bench}

A single $30$-question benchmark cannot separate a method from a lucky draw, so each
run's selected checkpoint is re-evaluated on six mathematics benchmarks
(Table~\ref{tab:bench}) under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:setup}: AIME24, AIME25
and AMC23 at $\mathrm{avg@}32$, MATH500 \citep{hendrycks2021measuring}, Minerva
\citep{lewkowycz2022solving} and OlympiadBench \citep{he2024olympiadbench} at
$\mathrm{avg@}1$.

An evaluation enters the table only if its log agrees with itself. The trainer prints
each benchmark twice on the same line, as accuracy and as the mean of a reward that is
$+1$ for a correct sample and $-1$ otherwise, so on every benchmark the accuracy must
equal half of one plus the reward; the release checks this for every evaluation log
(\texttt{scripts/check\_eval\_logs.py}). It is a test of coherence, not of provenance
--- an edit that moved both numbers together would pass it. Absent from the table,
for want of an evaluation that passes it: \num{Benchmissing}. Each row averages the
checkpoints evaluated at the seeds in its last column.

One of the six is not held out. The checkpoint behind each row is the argmax of that
run's AIME24 validation accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:valset}), so AIME24 is the set the
selection was made on and its column is in-sample: the pre-registration
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-prereg}) records that this argmax lifts an arm by about a
hundredth in absolute accuracy, and by an amount that differs between arms, so the
bias does not cancel cleanly inside a contrast. The column is marked $\dagger$ and
kept --- dropping a registered endpoint after seeing it is the worse of the two
errors --- and the five remaining benchmarks, which played no part in selection,
carry no such bias and only more variance.

The quantity fixed in advance as the headline here is not any single cell but

two or fewer evidence against. The observed count is \num{Dirconsistency} of six for

do not include one. Counting the benchmarks on the \num{Dirheldtotal} that played
no part in selection gives \num{Dirconsistencyheld}: the registered six-count is the decision rule, and the
held-out count is printed beside it so a reader can see how much of it survives

method from an artifact of one question set, and it is reported whatever it returns.

These two contrasts carry the attribution because each changes one thing:
\textsc{uniform} has the apparatus without $\AH$'s value, \textsc{permuted} has its
value without its pairing. The differences against GRPO and \textsc{steer} are
registered as well and appear in Table~\ref{tab:contrast}, but they change three
things and two respectively, so a benchmark difference there names no cause. Arms are
paired inside a seed throughout, for the reason Table~\ref{tab:plateau}'s caption
gives; the arm-mean rows of Table~\ref{tab:bench} are not what is being counted.

\paragraph{The paired error bar.} A single measurement of one arm on AIME24's $30$
questions carries an across-problem standard error of $\num{SEaime}$, which is why
the gaps in Table~\ref{tab:contrast} are a fraction of one problem in absolute terms.
That figure is the wrong one for a contrast: the two arms answer the same questions,
so per-problem difficulty cancels between them and what matters is the standard error
of the \emph{paired} difference. This evaluation records one row per generated
sample, so on the three $\mathrm{avg@}1$ benchmarks with enough problems to support
it --- MATH500, OlympiadBench and Minerva, $\num{Pairn}$ problems together, over the
seeds the arms share ($\num{Pairnseeds}$) --- the paired quantity is computed rather
than asserted. $\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{uniform}$ is $\num{Pairsigneduniform} \pm
\num{Pairsigneduniformse}$ ($t = \num{Pairsigneduniformt}$) against an unpaired error
of $\num{Pairsigneduniformunpse}$; the \textsc{permuted} contrast has no common
seed and so no paired figure. How much pairing buys is decided by how often the two
arms disagree on a problem, which is $\num{Pairsigneduniformdisc}$ here; the
measurement is what we report, not the reduction we wanted. Two cautions travel with
it. At $\mathrm{avg@}1$ each problem's score is a single Bernoulli draw, so these
errors carry the sampling noise of one generation per problem on top of the
difficulty spread; and pooling problems across seeds treats a (seed, problem) pair as
one observation, which ignores the clustering by seed that
Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds} shows is real. GSM8K is computed the same way and left
out of the pool: at $\mathrm{avg@}1$ it sits near saturation, where a paired
difference measures the ceiling rather than the method.

\subsection{How much of the design is load-bearing}
\label{sec:res:ablate}

Table~\ref{tab:followups} varies one element at a time, at a single seed per row.
Every comparison in it is therefore $n = 1$: these rows describe a mechanism, and
none of them carries an error bar or licenses an effect size. A row is read against
the main arm at \emph{its own} seed, which is not the same number as
Table~\ref{tab:plateau}'s across-seed mean.


\paragraph{The coefficient.} $\lambda$ scales the visitation term before the two
channels are combined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:combine}. The value used throughout was not
tuned on these runs, and the sweep exists to say whether the result rests on a knife
edge. A flat response across $0.1$--$0.5$ would say the method is insensitive to it;
a peak at $0.25$ with both neighbours below would say it is a tuned quantity and must
be reported as one.

\paragraph{The base method against GRPO, and what it does and does not reach.} We
measure \textsc{steer} below GRPO by $\num{Csteergrpoacc}$ over
$\num{Nseedsused}$ seeds, where the base method's own results place it above. We
read the released weighting function against ours and it agrees line for line, as
does every other value in the two launchers except $\lambda_{\min}$, which we set to
the paper's $0.7$ where the released script uses $0.8$. That difference does not
explain the gap on the base method's own evidence: both values lie in the range its
sensitivity analysis reports as performing consistently. The settings that do differ
from the base method's main experiments are the schedule ($110$ steps against its
$200$), the checkpoint rule (a fixed window against its best checkpoint) and the
number of runs, and we have no measurement that separates them. We did not run the
base method at the released $0.8$, so the question of why it lands below GRPO here
is left open rather than attributed.

What this discrepancy can reach is bounded, and by the design rather than by
luck. All four damped arms share $\lambda_{\min} = 0.7$ exactly, so the setting
cancels in any contrast between two of them. STEER-F $-$ \textsc{uniform} and STEER-F $-$
\textsc{permuted} --- the pair that carries this paper's central claim, that the
forecast's \emph{value} is what matters --- hold $\lambda_{\min}$, $\lambda$,
the sampler and the weight map all fixed and vary only what $\AH$ contains. Those
two do not depend on whatever puts the base method below GRPO here. What it does
reach is how two other contrasts read. \textsc{steer} $-$ GRPO is the discrepancy
itself. STEER-F $-$ \textsc{steer} is measured against a base method that is not
showing its reported gain over GRPO, so it is not a measurement of improvement on
top of that gain. We report both with this stated rather than resolved.

\paragraph{The sampler.} This is the cell no other arm supplies. Tree rollouts make a
group share prefixes, which changes the GRPO advantage estimate itself, quite apart
from any damping. \textsc{uniform} and \textsc{permuted} both run at $\lambda = .25$
and so cannot separate the sampler from the correction. The $\lambda = 0$ tree arm
switches the forecast off while leaving the sampler in place, and is the direct
answer to ``is tree rollout simply a better sampler''. Read against \textsc{steer},
which has neither, its difference is the sampler's own contribution; read against
STEER-F, what remains is the forecast's.

The answer is not the one the rest of this paper was written expecting. The sampler
alone reaches $\num{Rlamzeroacc}$ against \textsc{steer}'s $\num{Rsteeracc}$, and
$\num{Rlamzeromaj}$ against $\num{Rsteermaj}$ --- a little over half of STEER-F's
accuracy margin over \textsc{steer} and all of its majority-vote margin. Its uplift, $\num{Rlamzerouplift}$, is not below STEER-F's
$\num{Rsigneduplift}$. What remains for the forecast is the accuracy gap between
$\num{Rlamzeroacc}$ and $\num{Rsignedacc}$.

Three things keep this from being decisive, and none of them rescues the original
reading. Every cell here is one run, and the spread across this block is about twice
the run-to-run spread measured in Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds} --- suggestive, not
settled. The $\lambda$ sweep is not monotone: $\lambda = .1$ sits below
$\lambda = 0$ on accuracy, which is what noise looks like at this sample size. And
the row ran on the $\num{Rlamzerogpus}$-GPU box while the seed-1 references it is
read against did not, so the $\num{Boxdelta}$ of Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds} sits
inside this comparison and points the same way as the conclusion drawn from it: the
sampler's share is an upper bound, not an estimate. We
report the block as measured and do not reconcile it. Whether the sampler or the
forecast carries the structural term needs these arms at more seeds, which this
paper does not have. Nothing here establishes that the tree does not carry it, and
Section~\ref{sec:res:contrasts} is explicit that the forecast's \emph{value} does
not survive its own registered test. We do not turn this cell into a claim about the
sampler either: the schedule was fixed in advance to give $\AH$ its siblings, it was
not designed or tuned as a sampler, and whether structured sampling improves RLVR
in its own right is a different question from the one this paper asks.

\paragraph{The forecaster, replaced by what the trainer already has.}
Section~\ref{sec:res:locality} builds the same sibling-differenced quantity from the
policy's own next-token entropy and finds it close to $\AH$. That measurement is
taken on a frozen policy at one validation step, and it cannot say what the
substitution does \emph{inside} the loop, where the weights it produces move the
trajectory the next measurement would be taken on. The \textsc{oracle} arm makes the
substitution there: same tree, same $\lambda$, same weight map, with $\Htogo$ read
from the realised entropy of the rollout verl has already computed. A2 and A3 ---
the head substitution and the affine calibration --- are the only things it removes,
and removing them is the whole of its cost difference.

It is cheaper, and by the amount the forward pass costs: $\num{Roraclecost}$\,s per
optimization step against STEER-F's $\num{Fsignedoracleref}$ at the same seed, a
saving of $\num{Fsignedoraclesaving}\%$. Both are the window's training steps on the
four-GPU box, where every arm is substantially faster than on the two-GPU seeds, so
neither is comparable to a step time quoted for a different box.

It is also behind on accuracy. Over the
$\num{Fnsignedoracle}$ validation points of the converged window, STEER-F at seed
$\num{Roracleseed}$ leads it by $\num{Fsignedoracleacc}$ on the endpoint this
campaign reports and by $\num{Fsignedoraclemaj}$ under majority vote. We therefore
keep A2 and A3 rather than replacing the forecaster with the realised value, and we
state the weight of that decision exactly: one run on each side, one seed. The gap is
larger than STEER-F's own range across seeds, $\num{Ssignedacc}$, and smaller than
GRPO's, $\num{Sgrpoacc}$. It orders the two arms on this seed; it does not measure
the difference between them, and the measurement that would is this arm at more
seeds.

\paragraph{Clipping.} The relaxed-clipping setting is where entropy interventions
are supposed to matter most, because ratio clipping otherwise removes much of the
update a token weight would have scaled. The row is one STEER-F run with no
finished control under the same clipping, and it ran on a different machine from the
seed-$\num{Rxclipsignedseed}$ STEER-F reference, so it carries no contrast in either
direction. It shows that the method trains to the end of the schedule with the clip
relaxed; it does not say whether relaxing the clip changes what the method does.

\subsection{Compute-matched comparison}
\label{sec:res:compute}

An equal-step comparison is compute-fair only if the two arms cost the same per
step, and here that depends on the machine. On the $\num{Costgpushi}$-GPU box
(seeds $\num{Cmhiseeds}$) STEER-F's step costs $\num{Cmhiratio}$ times GRPO's,
so equal steps is equal compute to within the seed spread of either arm
(Section~\ref{sec:res:cost}). On the $\num{Costgpuslo}$-GPU box (seed
$\num{Cmloseeds}$) it costs $\num{Cmloratio}$ times as much, and a GRPO run given
the same wall clock would reach about step $\num{Cmlostep}$. We did not train GRPO
past step $110$, so that point is not measured, and we do not extrapolate a
trajectory to it.

What the split does measure is where the headline gap comes from, and it is not
evenly spread. $\text{STEER-F} - \text{GRPO}$ is $\num{Cmlosignedgrpoacc}$ on the
seed where STEER-F also had the larger compute budget, and $\num{Cmhisignedgrpoacc}$
averaged over the $\num{Cmhin}$ seeds where the comparison is compute-fair. The
across-seed mean of $\num{Csignedgrpoacc}$ in Table~\ref{tab:contrast} is
therefore carried mostly by a seed on which the treatment was also granted about
half as much again in wall clock, and on the compute-fair seeds the gap is not
distinguishable from zero. We report this as the reading the evidence supports:
this paper does not show that STEER-F buys accuracy over GRPO at equal compute.
None of this touches the contrasts that carry the structural claim ---
$\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{uniform}$ and $\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{permuted}$ ---
whose arms share the tree sampler and cost $\num{Costsignedhi}$,
$\num{Costuniformhi}$ and $\num{Costpermutedhi}$\,s per step on the larger box.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:res:cost}

Step times are read inside a box. A seed index also names a machine
(Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds}), and the two boxes are further apart than any two arms
are, so a mean over both measures the campaign's scheduling.

On the $\num{Costgpushi}$-GPU box, where every arm has finished
$\num{Costnsignedhi}$ seeds, one optimization step takes $\num{Coststeerhi}$\,s for
\textsc{steer}, $\num{Costsignedhi}$ for STEER-F, $\num{Costuniformhi}$ for
\textsc{uniform}, $\num{Costpermutedhi}$ for \textsc{permuted} and
$\num{Costgrpohi}$ for GRPO. STEER-F's overhead over \textsc{steer} there is
$\num{Costoverheadhi}\%$. The three tree arms take $\num{Costsignedhi}$, $\num{Costuniformhi}$ and
$\num{Costpermutedhi}$\,s while \textsc{steer}, which has neither the tree nor the
heads, sits below all three: the cost is the rollout and the forward
pass, not the $\AH$ computation, which is a subtraction over a mask.

On the $\num{Costgpuslo}$-GPU box the same overhead reads
$\num{Costoverheadlo}\%$. That is not a disagreement to average away. The tree
rollout and the MTP forward are a larger share of a step when fewer GPUs serve it, so
the overhead is a property of the deployment as much as of the method, and a single
figure quoted without its box --- as earlier drafts of this section quoted the smaller
one --- reports the machine.

Neither figure is resolved to better than the spread underneath it. On the larger box
\textsc{steer}'s $\num{Costnsteerhi}$ seeds differ by $\num{Costrsteerhi}$\,s and
STEER-F's by $\num{Costrsignedhi}$, against a gap between their means of the same
order. On the smaller box every arm has $\num{Costnsteerlo}$ seed inside
$\num{Seedset}$, so there is no spread to put beside it at all. We therefore
report these as the cost of the runs we made and not as a coefficient of the method.
What does isolate the heads from the tree is the \textsc{oracle} arm of
Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate}, which removes the heads at fixed tree and fixed seed.

\subsection{A note on how these numbers were selected}
\label{sec:res:selection}

We report window means rather than the best checkpoint, and the difference is not
cosmetic. Selecting the maximum over the twelve validation points inflates every arm,
averaged over the $\num{Nseedsused}$ seeds: by $\num{Rgrposellift}$ for GRPO,
$\num{Rsteersellift}$ for \textsc{steer}, $\num{Runiformsellift}$ for
\textsc{uniform}, $\num{Rpermutedsellift}$ for \textsc{permuted} and
$\num{Rsignedsellift}$ for STEER-F, relative to the window mean.
Since a single AIME24 measurement carries a standard error of $\num{SEaime}$ across
problems, a maximum over a dozen noisy points is a biased statistic whose bias grows
with the number of checkpoints compared, and it is not the same for every arm: the
lifts differ by more than the gap between \textsc{uniform} and \textsc{permuted},
so ranking by the maximum would swap those two. We regard the window mean as the
defensible statistic and use it throughout. The one exception is
Section~\ref{sec:reported}, which uses the maximum on purpose, because that is the
rule of the table it is compared against.


\section{Against the Reported Numbers}
\label{sec:reported}

Every number before this section is our own measurement under the schedule of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}, which differs from the base method's in length, checkpoint
rule and number of runs (Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate}). This section is the one place
a figure is taken from prior work. It runs the full method under the base method's own
protocol and sets the result beside that method's published $1.5$B table
\citep[Table~12]{steer}, which also reports GRPO and the untrained model. It is a
second kind of evidence, not a sixth arm, and we say below what it cannot show.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Two independent runs, with data seeds $1$ and $2$, of $200$
optimization steps each; AIME24 validation every $10$ steps; in each run the
checkpoint with the highest AIME24 accuracy is selected and evaluated on the six
benchmarks of Section~\ref{sec:res:bench}, and a table entry is the mean over the two
runs. Each of these items is the base method's \citep[App.~E]{steer}. Everything else
is the configuration of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, with one change: the tree sampler
uses two roots, each cut at depths $\{64, 192\}$ with branching factor $2$, so the
eight responses to a prompt form two independent groups of four instead of one group
that shares its first $64$ tokens. The purpose of the change was to keep the
siblings the estimator needs while sharing less of each response, because short runs
after the five-arm campaign showed the single-root tree making the eight rewards of a
group tie more often, which removes signal from a GRPO update. It is an adjustment to
keep the estimator's support affordable, not an attempt to improve the sampler. The
choice is exploratory, it was made on training prompts and not on any benchmark, and
these runs are not one of the five arms. The selected
steps are \num{ProtostepBestOne} and \num{ProtostepBestTwo}.


Each AIME24 cell of ours is read from the training log and used only if it agrees
with the reward printed beside it and with the trainer's own record of the best
value; the other columns pass the coherence check of Section~\ref{sec:res:bench}
(\texttt{scripts/emit\_r2\_numbers.py}). The two-run AIME24 mean is averaged over
correct-sample counts rather than over two rounded fractions.

\paragraph{Reading the table.} Averaged over the two runs, STEER-F's six-benchmark
mean is \num{ProtobestMeanAvg}, against \num{ProtorefSteerAvg} for the base method and
\num{ProtorefGrpoAvg} for GRPO as reported; on AIME24 the same three figures are
\num{ProtobestMeanAime}, \num{ProtorefSteerAime} and \num{ProtorefGrpoAime}. One
AIME24 evaluation carries an across-problem standard error of \num{SEaime} as a
fraction (Section~\ref{sec:valset}); a mean of two runs halves its variance and no
more, and the quoted rows carry a noise of their own that their source does not
state. Against the base method's reported row the two-run mean is lower on
\num{ProtocmpSteerBelow} of the six columns and not lower on
\num{ProtocmpSteerNotbelow}, the column the selection itself used; against the
reported GRPO row it is higher on \num{ProtocmpGrpoAbove}. We therefore read the
table as placing the method between the two reported rows and below the base method
as reported, not level with it; how much of that gap belongs to the method and how
much to the environment, an uncontrolled comparison cannot say. The step-$200$ row is
printed because the selected checkpoint is chosen on AIME24 and so flatters its own
row; the last checkpoint is chosen by nobody.

\paragraph{What this comparison is not.} It is not controlled. The quoted rows ran
with the authors' code on different hardware (a cluster of H20 GPUs, against our four
H100s), and the base method under this protocol in our environment is a run this
paper does not have. The controlled comparison with the base method is the five-arm
campaign, where $\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{steer}$ is \num{Csignedsteeracc} over
\num{Nsignedsteer} paired seeds at $110$ steps (Table~\ref{tab:contrast}), with the
caveats of Section~\ref{sec:res:ablate}. Nor does the table speak to the visitation
channel: like every comparison between the full and the base method
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}), it changes the sampler and the correction together, and
the attribution rests on the controls of Section~\ref{sec:results} alone.



\section{Closed Forms for Section~\ref{sec:blind}}
\label{sec:appendix-closed}

Perturbing one logit of a $k$-way uniform distribution by $\delta$ and writing
$S := e^{\delta} + k - 1$ gives $\pi_1 = e^{\delta}/S$ and $\pi_j = 1/S$ for
$j \neq 1$, so
%
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
H(\delta) &= \log S - \frac{\delta e^{\delta}}{S},\\
H'(\delta) &= -\frac{(k-1)\,\delta\, e^{\delta}}{S^{2}},
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
%
whence $H'(0) = 0$ and $H''(0) = -(k-1)/k^{2}$. The sign of $H'$ is
$-\operatorname{sign}(\delta)$, so any perturbation lowers the entropy, but only at
second order. Table~\ref{tab:secondorder} contrasts this with a peaked distribution,
where the first-order term does not vanish.

distribution & $H_0$ & $\Om$ & $\Delta H$ at $\delta{=}.1$ \\
\midrule

uniform, $k{=}50$      & 3.912 & $0$      & $-1.0\times10^{-4}$ \\

\section{Implementation Notes}
\label{sec:appendix-impl}

\paragraph{Sibling mask.} For a group of $G$ rollouts of length $T$ we form
$\mathrm{eq}[i,j,t] = \mathbf{1}[y^{(i)}_t = y^{(j)}_t]$ and take a cumulative
product along $t$, so that $\mathrm{prefixeq}[i,j,t]$ is true exactly when the two
rollouts agree on $y_{\le t}$. Shifting it one position right gives agreement on
$y_{<t}$, which is the sibling relation Eq.~\eqref{eq:ah} requires. Padded positions
are treated as disagreement, so a length difference counts as a divergence.

\paragraph{Alignment.} $\hat{H}_{\mathrm{togo}}$ is read at position $t+1$ and moved
to position $t$; at the final position the value is kept. Omitting this shift scores
the state before the branch instead of the branch taken.

\paragraph{Memory.} The RL path never materializes head logits. For each head the
hidden states are processed in chunks of $4096$ rows, mapped through the shared
unembedding, reduced to an entropy, and released, so peak memory is
$O(\text{chunk} \times V)$ rather than $O(\kappa \times B \times T \times V)$.

\paragraph{Equivalence test.} A unit test asserts that with $\lambda = 0$ the token
weights are bitwise identical to the base method's on random inputs; the
$\lambda = 0$ early return in the combination step is what makes this hold exactly
rather than to floating-point tolerance.

\section{Pre-Registered Analysis}
\label{sec:appendix-prereg}

The analysis plan below was committed to the artifact repository before any run of
the multi-seed campaign was launched, so that the statistics could not be chosen to
fit the outcome. It is reproduced here in full; the file is
\texttt{docs/preregistration\_5seed.md} in the release.

\paragraph{Unit and primary endpoint.} The unit of analysis is the seed, not the
validation step. Each run is reduced to one number --- its mean $\mathrm{acc@}32$ on
AIME24 over steps $40$--$110$ --- and arms are paired within a seed.

\paragraph{Contrasts, in the order they are reported.} Primary: $\text{STEER-F} -
\text{GRPO}$. Secondary, in this order: $\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{steer}$,
$\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{uniform}$, $\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{permuted}$. Secondary
endpoints: $\mathrm{maj@}32$, uplift, and the six-benchmark mean. Effect sizes are
reported with intervals rather than significance stars, and all five arms are
reported whatever the outcome.

\paragraph{Stopping and exclusion.} Every run goes to step $110$; none is stopped
early for looking good or bad. A run is excluded only for mechanical failure --- out
of memory, a crash, a corrupted checkpoint, or a step-$0$ validation score outside
$.030$--$.055$, which would indicate a different initial checkpoint. Exclusions are
listed with their reason. A failed run is relaunched with the same seed, never a new
one, and no seed is added after results are seen.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection.} Two rules are computed and both reported: the
argmax over a run's validation points, and a fixed step $110$. The six-benchmark
evaluation uses the argmax. Selecting a maximum over twelve noisy checkpoints is
biased upward --- in the single-seed data by $+.011$ to $+.017$ per arm --- which is
why the primary endpoint is a window mean rather than a maximum.

\paragraph{What would falsify the central claim.} Stated in advance so it cannot be
renegotiated afterwards. If $\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{uniform}$ straddles zero across
seeds, the forecast's \emph{value} does not matter and the apparatus explains the
effect; that is the claim of this paper and it would be refuted. If $\text{STEER-F} -
\textsc{permuted}$ straddles zero, the pairing between a branch and its score does
not matter, which the derivation says it must. If aggregate entropy orders the arms
the same way accuracy does, the dissociation argument of
Section~\ref{sec:res:entropy} fails, since it depends on the two orderings coming
apart. And if the mean token weight in the STEER-F arm departs from $\approx 0.999$,
then $\AH$ is not sum-zero over sibling sets as derived and the implementation is
wrong rather than the theory.

\paragraph{Amendment, recorded.} The pre-registration assigned three seeds to
\textsc{uniform} and \textsc{permuted} and five to the other three arms. The campaign
as executed runs all five arms at five seeds. The change raises the power of the two
control contrasts and was made before any result of the campaign was seen; it is
recorded here rather than silently absorbed.

\paragraph{Deviations as executed.} Recorded here because the plan above was
not carried out in full. (i) Seeds: every arm completed seeds $1$--$4$ except
STEER-F at seed $2$, which failed at start-up and was not relaunched; the seed-$5$
runs were started and not completed. The analysis therefore uses the seeds on which
all five arms finished, $\num{Seedset}$, and the other runs are released but enter
no statistic. (ii) Machines: seed $1$ ran on a two-GPU box and seeds $3$ and $4$ on a
four-GPU box, for every arm (Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds}). (iii) The six-benchmark
evaluation covers one seed per arm and no \textsc{steer} checkpoint
(Section~\ref{sec:res:bench}). (iv) The long GRPO run for the compute-matched
comparison was not completed; Section~\ref{sec:res:compute} reports what the step
costs support instead. (v) The planned second backbones were not run. (vi) The
support-restricted split registered below is instrumented but carried by no run
reported here. (vii) Follow-up arms: the four advantage-estimator runs (RLOO and
OPO, each with STEER-F and \textsc{steer}) failed at start-up on infrastructure
errors and completed no training step; the base method at the released
$\lambda_{\min} = 0.8$ was not launched; the relaxed-clipping \textsc{steer} control
stopped at step $\num{Rxclipsteerlaststep}$. None of them enters
Table~\ref{tab:followups}, and their logs are released. The choice to analyse on the common seed set was made after
per-seed results were visible; it is the only set on which all five arms exist, and

\paragraph{Second amendment, added 2026-09-21, after this section's own entropy
condition was retired.} Everything above was fixed before the campaign ran. This
paragraph was not, and it is marked so rather than folded into the text above. It
justifies nothing about the seeds already collected; it binds the seeds not yet in
hand.

\emph{What it replaces.} The third falsification bullet above put a condition on the
aggregate entropy ordering. Section~\ref{sec:res:entropy} retires it as a category
error --- it registers the aggregate as a signal about $\AH$, which
Section~\ref{sec:zerosum} had already said it cannot be, an hour before this file was
committed --- and claims nothing from it in either direction. A first attempt at a
replacement, registered earlier on 2026-09-21, named
\texttt{steerf/branch\_entropy}; that registration is withdrawn here rather than
deleted, because the monitor it named selects a fixed top decile of $\AH$ rather than
the support (Section~\ref{sec:res:diag}).

\emph{Registered now, before the runs that can answer it.} The quantity is
\texttt{steerf/support\_entropy} and its gap, the entropy split on $\AH \ne 0$ ---
the same predicate the correction itself applies, so the split covers the positions
the method touches and no others. The primary contrast is
$\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{permuted}$, paired by seed, because that pair shares
$\lambda$, $\lambda_{\min}$, the sampler and the magnitudes of $\AH$, and differs
only in whether a branch is paired with its own score;
$\text{STEER-F} - \textsc{uniform}$ is secondary. The claim under test is that a
support-restricted entropy contrast near zero can accompany an accuracy contrast away
from zero.

\emph{What would falsify it.} If the support-restricted contrast tracks the accuracy
contrast in sign and magnitude across seeds the way the apparatus contrasts in
Table~\ref{tab:entropy} do, then the split is the aggregate statistic in a smaller
window and the claim fails.

\emph{What is still not instrumented, and we say so rather than substitute.}
Section~\ref{sec:zerosum}'s own prediction is that the spread \emph{across siblings}
widens. Testing it needs the sibling index at the loss site, where only the
per-token tensors are currently in scope, so no quantity we log tests it today. We
do not offer the support-restricted split as that test: it measures a level, not a
spread.

\section{Within-Run Stability}
\label{sec:appendix-within}

Earlier drafts of this work paired the arms at each of the eight validation steps in
the converged window and reported a paired $t$ over those eight points. That
statistic answers a narrower question than the one in
Section~\ref{sec:res:contrasts}: it asks whether a gap is stable along a single
trajectory, not whether it reappears when the run is repeated. Its $n$ counts
validation points, which are serially correlated within a run, so it is not an
estimate of run-to-run uncertainty and cannot be read as one.

We report it here because it is informative about a different thing --- whether a gap
is an artifact of one lucky validation point --- and because the single-seed version
of this manuscript quoted it. Where the two disagree, the seed-level statistic in
Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds} governs.

The contrast between the two is the point. Table~\ref{tab:within} separates every
$\AH$ comparison at $t_7$ between $\num{WTsignedpermutedacc}$ and
$\num{WTsigneduniformacc}$, which reads as decisive and is not: the same gap sits at
the same order as the run-to-run spread we measure in
Section~\ref{sec:res:seeds}. A statistic can be this confident about a trajectory
and say nothing about a re-run, which is exactly why we moved it out of the main
